\title{Contingent Logical Truths, Mechanized:\\
Actuality and Real-World Validity in a Lean~4 Situation Theory}
\author{Haniel Ulises}
\date{November 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Zalta argued that a modal language with an actuality operator contains
logical truths that are not necessary, such as
$\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi$. The argument identifies logical
truth with \emph{real-world validity}, truth at the actual world of
every model, rather than with \emph{general validity}, truth at every
world of every model. We give a mechanization of this argument in
Lean~4 at three levels. First, in a deep embedding of propositional
modal logic with actuality, we prove for an arbitrary class of models
that general validity is closed under necessitation while real-world
validity is not, that $\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi$ is
real-world valid for every $\varphi$ but neither it nor its
necessitation is valid in the stronger sense in any class containing a
two-world model, and that $\Box(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)$
holds at a world exactly when $\varphi$ takes its actual value
throughout that world's accessible worlds. Second, we add an actuality
operator, as a definition that introduces no axioms, to a Lean
development of situation and world theory in the style of Zalta, and
prove that the deep and shallow semantics of $\Act$ agree. Third, we
give a concrete model of all 48 axioms of that theory, checked against
them mechanically, in which the actuality truth for a contingent
proposition holds at the actual world while its necessitation fails.
We also report how earlier versions of the theory's axioms collapsed,
and how separating situated truth from obtaining repairs them.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Is every logical truth necessary? The traditional answer is yes, and
modal logics reflect it in the rule of necessitation: from
$\vdash\varphi$ infer $\vdash\Box\varphi$. Zalta~\cite{zalta1988}
argued that the answer is no once the language contains a device for
referring to the actual world. Let $\Act$ be an actuality operator, so
that $\Act\varphi$ is true at any world just in case $\varphi$ is true
at the actual world. Then $\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi$ is true
at the actual world of every interpretation, and so, on the natural
model-theoretic account of logical truth, it is a logical truth. Yet
it is not necessary: if $\varphi$ is actually true but could have been
false, then at a world where $\varphi$ fails, $\Act\varphi$ still
holds, so the biconditional fails there.

The formal ingredients go back to Crossley and
Humberstone~\cite{crossley1977}, and the two notions of validity
involved were named by Davies and Humberstone~\cite{davies1980}:
\emph{real-world validity} (truth at the designated actual world of
every model) and \emph{general validity} (truth at every world of
every model). Zalta's claim is that real-world validity, not general
validity, is the correct explication of logical truth. Hanson~\cite{hanson2006}
defended the traditional link between logical truth and necessity, and
Nelson and Zalta~\cite{nelson2012} replied.

The philosophical dispute is about which notion of validity explicates
logical truth. The formal facts on which both sides rely should not be
in dispute, but they are subtle enough that it is worth having them
checked by machine. This paper does so in Lean~4~\cite{demoura2021}.
Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item A deep embedding of propositional modal logic with $\Box$ and
  $\Act$, with real-world and general validity defined relative to an
  arbitrary class of models, and machine-checked proofs of the closure
  properties, non-closure under necessitation, strictness, and a
  characterization of when $\Box(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)$
  holds (Section~\ref{sec:deep}).
\item An actuality operator in an existing Lean development of
  situation and world theory, defined rather than postulated, with a
  proof that the development is a model of the deep embedding and that
  the two treatments of $\Act$ agree (Section~\ref{sec:theory}).
\item A concrete, non-degenerate model of all 48 axioms of the
  situation theory, in which the actuality truth for a contingent
  proposition holds but is not necessary, together with a check that
  ties the model's statements to the axioms (Section~\ref{sec:model}).
\item A report on how earlier versions of the theory's axioms
  collapsed, and on the repair that makes room for the results above
  (Section~\ref{sec:repair}).
\end{enumerate}

All results are proved without \lean{sorry}. The development is
available at
\url{https://github.com/HanielUlises/Possible-World-Semantics}.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{The logic of actuality.}
A model for propositional modal logic with actuality is a Kripke model
$M = \langle W, R, \aw, V\rangle$ with a designated actual world
$\aw \in W$. Truth at a world is defined as usual, with
\[
  M, w \models \Act\varphi \quad\text{iff}\quad M, \aw \models \varphi .
\]
A formula is \emph{real-world valid} in a class $\mathcal{C}$ of models
if it is true at $\aw$ in every $M \in \mathcal{C}$, and \emph{generally
valid} in $\mathcal{C}$ if it is true at every world of every
$M \in \mathcal{C}$. General validity implies real-world validity. The
converse fails, and so does closure of real-world validity under
necessitation. Both facts are witnessed by
$\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi$.

\paragraph{Situation and world theory.}
Zalta's theory of abstract objects~\cite{zalta1983,zalta1988book}
distinguishes \emph{exemplifying} a property from \emph{encoding} it.
Situations are abstract objects that encode only propositional
properties, and a proposition is true in a situation when the
situation encodes the corresponding property. Worlds are situations
that could make true exactly the propositions that are true. On this
basis Zalta proved twenty-five basic theorems of situation and world
theory~\cite{zalta1993}, and Fitelson and Zalta verified a number of
them with the automated reasoner Prover9~\cite{fitelson2007}.

\paragraph{The Lean development.}
The development extended here formalizes a version of this theory in
Lean~4 as a shallow embedding. A single type \lean{World} serves as
the domain of objects, situations and indices. Encoding (\lean{Enc}),
situated truth ($s \models p$) and situationhood are primitives
constrained by axioms; parthood $s \trianglelefteq s'$ is inclusion of
encoded properties; and $\Box$ and $\Diamond$ are \emph{defined} by
quantification over a universal accessibility relation, so that the S5
principles are theorems. Every axiom is listed in an inventory, and the
development has no axioms beyond the 48 listed there.

\section{The logic of actuality, deeply embedded}
\label{sec:deep}

We define formulas over an arbitrary type of atoms and models with a
designated actual world.

\begin{lstlisting}
inductive Formula (α : Type) where
  | atom : α → Formula α
  | bot  : Formula α
  | neg  : Formula α → Formula α
  | conj : Formula α → Formula α → Formula α
  | impl : Formula α → Formula α → Formula α
  | box  : Formula α → Formula α
  | act  : Formula α → Formula α

structure Model (α : Type) where
  W      : Type
  R      : W → W → Prop
  actual : W
  val    : α → W → Prop
\end{lstlisting}

Truth at a world, \lean{sat M φ w}, is defined by structural recursion
on the formula; the clause for $\Act$ ignores the world of evaluation:

\begin{lstlisting}
  | box φ, w => ∀ v, M.R w v → sat M φ v
  | act φ, _ => sat M φ M.actual
\end{lstlisting}

A class of models is a predicate on models. The classes we use are all
models, models whose accessibility relation is an equivalence (S5), and
models whose accessibility relation is universal. The two notions of
validity are:

\begin{lstlisting}
def RWValid (C : ModelClass α) (φ : Formula α) : Prop :=
  ∀ M, C M → sat M φ M.actual

def GValid (C : ModelClass α) (φ : Formula α) : Prop :=
  ∀ M, C M → ∀ w, sat M φ w
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{proposition}[closure]
For every class $\mathcal{C}$: general validity implies real-world
validity (\lean{GValid.rwValid}); both are closed under modus ponens
(\lean{RWValid.mp}, \lean{GValid.mp}); and general validity is closed
under necessitation (\lean{GValid.nec}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[the actuality truth]
For every class $\mathcal{C}$ and every formula $\varphi$,
$\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi$ is real-world valid in
$\mathcal{C}$ (\lean{actuality\_rwValid}).
\end{proposition}

The countermodel has two mutually accessible worlds, \lean{true}
(actual) and \lean{false}, and makes every atom true at \lean{true}
only. It belongs to the S5 and universal classes
(\lean{twoWorlds\_S5}, \lean{twoWorlds\_universal}). At the world
\lean{false}, an atom $a$ is false but $\Act a$ is true.

\begin{theorem}[non-closure under necessitation]
\label{thm:nonclosure}
Let $\mathcal{C}$ contain the two-world model and let $a$ be an atom.
Then $a \leftrightarrow \Act a$ is not generally valid in $\mathcal{C}$
(\lean{actuality\_not\_gValid}), and
$\Box(a \leftrightarrow \Act a)$ is not even real-world valid in
$\mathcal{C}$ (\lean{box\_actuality\_not\_rwValid}). Hence real-world
validity is strictly weaker than general validity
(\lean{rwValid\_strictly\_weaker}) and is not closed under necessitation
(\lean{rwValid\_not\_closed\_under\_nec}).
\end{theorem}

In Lean, the S5 instance of the second statement reads:

\begin{lstlisting}
theorem contingent_logical_truth_S5 (a : α) :
    ∃ φ : Formula α, RWValid S5Models φ ∧ ¬ RWValid S5Models (box φ)
\end{lstlisting}

The failure of necessitation is not a feature of the particular atom.
It is exactly as wide as contingency.

\begin{theorem}[when the necessitation holds]
\label{thm:characterization}
For every model $M$, formula $\varphi$ and world $w$,
\[
  M, w \models \Box(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)
  \quad\text{iff}\quad
  \text{for all } v \text{ with } wRv,\;
  M, v \models \varphi \iff M, \aw \models \varphi
\]
(\lean{sat\_box\_actuality}). If $R$ is universal, the right-hand side
says that $\varphi$ is true everywhere or false everywhere
(\lean{sat\_box\_actuality\_universal}).
\end{theorem}

Finally, actuality is inert for real-world validity but not for general
validity.

\begin{proposition}[inertness]
For every class $\mathcal{C}$: $\Act\varphi$ is real-world valid iff
$\varphi$ is (\lean{rwValid\_act\_iff}); $\Act\varphi$ is generally
valid iff $\varphi$ is \emph{real-world} valid (\lean{gValid\_act\_iff});
and $\Act\varphi \to \Box\Act\varphi$ is generally valid in all models
(\lean{act\_imp\_box\_act\_gValid}).
\end{proposition}

The second clause is a compact statement of the whole phenomenon.
Prefixing $\Act$ converts real-world validity into general validity,
so $\Act(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)$ is generally valid and
hence necessary, while $\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi$ itself is
not. What is necessary is that the biconditional \emph{actually}
holds, not that it holds.

All proofs in this section use only Lean's logic. For the S5 result
above, \lean{\#print axioms} lists only Lean's standard axioms
\lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice} and \lean{Quot.sound}.

\section{Actuality in situation theory}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Situated truth and obtaining}

The situation theory distinguishes two truth relations. Situated truth
$s \models p$ is partial: a situation may make neither $p$ nor $\neg p$
true. The relation $\Obt\ v\ p$, read ``$p$ is the case at index
$v$'', is classical; it alone is governed by the clauses
\[
  \Obt\ v\ (\neg p) \leftrightarrow \neg\,\Obt\ v\ p,
  \qquad
  \Obt\ v\ (p \wedge q) \leftrightarrow \Obt\ v\ p \wedge \Obt\ v\ q,
\]
and similarly for disjunction and implication. Following Zalta, a world
is a situation that could make true exactly what is the case, and a
situation is actual when everything true in it is actually the case:
\begin{align*}
  \mathsf{World}(s) &:= \mathsf{Situation}(s) \wedge
    \Diamond\lambda v.\,\forall p\,(s \models p \leftrightarrow \Obt\ v\ p),\\
  \mathsf{Actual}(s) &:= \forall p\,(s \models p \to \Obt\ \aw\ p).
\end{align*}
With these definitions the classical behaviour of truth at worlds, and
the maximality and consistency of worlds, are theorems; worlds are
characterized as exactly the maximal possible situations. The designated
constant \lean{actualWorld} is used both as a situation and as the
index of evaluation, and a single axiom, \lean{actualWorld\_faithful},
states that its truths are exactly what obtains at it.

\subsection{The actuality operator}

Because $\Box$, $\Diamond$ and \lean{actualWorld} are already available,
$\Act$ can be defined rather than postulated:

\begin{lstlisting}
def Actually (p : World → Prop) : World → Prop :=
  fun _ => p actualWorld

notation "𝒜" => Actually
\end{lstlisting}

The actuality truth then holds at the actual world by definition, for
every condition $p$ on indices (\lean{actuality\_truth}). Its
necessitation is governed by the same characterization as in the deep
embedding.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:theory}
For every $p$ and $w$, $\Box(p \leftrightarrow \Act p)$ holds at $w$
iff $p$ is necessary or impossible at $w$
(\lean{box\_actuality\_truth\_iff}). Consequently, if $p$ is contingent
at $w$ (possibly true and possibly false), then
$\Box(p \leftrightarrow \Act p)$ fails at $w$:
\end{theorem}

\begin{lstlisting}
theorem contingent_actuality_truth_not_necessary
    (p : World → Prop) (w : World) (hc : Contingent p w) :
    ¬ □ (fun v => p v ↔ 𝒜 p v) w
\end{lstlisting}

Further theorems record that $\Act$ is rigid, idempotent, and necessary
when true ($\Box\Act p \leftrightarrow \Act p$ and
$\Diamond\Act p \leftrightarrow \Act p$), and that the actuality of a
situation is the actualization of its possibility condition:
$\mathsf{Actual}(s)$ holds iff
$\Act(\lambda v.\,\forall p\,(s \models p \to \Obt\ v\ p))$ holds at any
index (\lean{actual\_iff\_actually}).

\subsection{The development as a model of the logic}

The development can be read as a model of the deep embedding: its
worlds are the indices, accessibility is the development's relation
$R$, the actual world is \lean{actualWorld}, and an atom is a
proposition, true at an index where it obtains. A translation
\lean{interp} sends each formula to a condition on worlds, interpreting
$\Box$ as the development's \lean{Box} and $\Act$ as \lean{Actually}.

\begin{theorem}[agreement]
For every formula $\varphi$ and world $v$,
$\lean{sat theoryModel}\ \varphi\ v \leftrightarrow \lean{interp}\ \varphi\ v$
(\lean{sat\_theoryModel}). Since \lean{theoryModel} is universal, every
formula that is real-world valid over universal models holds at the
actual world of the development (\lean{rwValid\_holds\_actually}).
\end{theorem}

The theorem is proved by induction on formulas; the $\Act$ case is
exactly the definition of \lean{Actually}. It shows that the shallow
operator is not an ad hoc device but the operator of the
Crossley--Humberstone semantics.

\section{A model of the whole theory}
\label{sec:model}

The theory itself does not settle whether any proposition is
contingent: nothing in the axioms forces there to be more than one
index. To show that the necessitation of the actuality truth can fail
in the presence of all the axioms of situation theory, and not merely in
a bare Kripke model, we give a model of the whole theory.

\paragraph{The structure.}
There are two valuations, \lean{true} (actual) and \lean{false}. A
proposition is a set of valuations, so the connectives are Boolean
operations and the propositional identities among the axioms hold.
Properties are propositions. Objects are either indices, one per
valuation, which are concrete and encode nothing (the ordinary
objects), or abstract objects, one per set of properties (the
situations). A situation makes $p$ true iff it encodes $p$, and
$\Obt\ v\ p$ evaluates $p$ at the valuation of $v$. The actual world is
the abstract object encoding exactly the propositions true at
\lean{true}.

\paragraph{Faithfulness.}
The model file imports nothing from the development. Each primitive is
given a concrete definition under its original name, each definition
that an axiom mentions is copied verbatim, and each of the 48 axioms is
restated verbatim as a theorem and proved. A script compares the
statements, the copied definitions and the notations with those of the
development, up to whitespace, and fails if any differ; we checked
that it detects a changed statement and a changed notation precedence.
Since every axiom is true in the model, no contradiction is derivable
from the axioms.

\paragraph{Non-degeneracy.}
The model has two distinct worlds, a proposition that is possibly the
case and possibly not, a partial situation strictly below a world that
is possible but not itself a world, an impossible situation, and
ordinary objects alongside abstract ones (\lean{Model.nondegenerate}).
In it, the actuality truth for the contingent proposition holds at the
actual world while its necessitation fails:

\begin{lstlisting}
theorem contingent_logical_truth :
    (fun w => Obtains w contingentProp ↔
      𝒜 (fun v => Obtains v contingentProp) w) actualWorld ∧
    ¬ □ (fun w => Obtains w contingentProp ↔
      𝒜 (fun v => Obtains v contingentProp) w) actualWorld
\end{lstlisting}

The witness for the failure is the index for the valuation
\lean{false}, where the proposition does not obtain although it
actually does.

\section{How the theory collapsed, and the repair}
\label{sec:repair}

The results above depend on a version of the situation theory that
differs from earlier ones in the repository. Those earlier versions were
not rejected by the type checker, but they were degenerate. We found
the degeneracy by auditing the axioms and deriving its consequences in
Lean, and the model of Section~\ref{sec:model} now rules it out. We record the failures because they illustrate pitfalls
of shallow embeddings of metaphysical theories that are easy to miss.

\paragraph{Classical clauses for partial truth.}
The clauses $s \models \neg p \leftrightarrow \neg(s \models p)$, and
those for the other connectives, were stated for every situation $s$.
This forces every situation to decide every proposition, so no
situation is partial. Worse, together with the axiom giving the truth
conditions of the meet $s \wedge s'$ of two situations, it forces any
two indices to agree on every proposition: computing the truth of
$\neg p$ at $s \wedge s'$ in the two available ways gives two answers
that coincide only if $s$ and $s'$ agree on $p$. With extensionality,
there was at most one situation.

\paragraph{Truth simpliciter replaced by \texttt{True}.}
Zalta's definition of worldhood,
$\Diamond\forall p\,(s \models p \leftrightarrow p)$, uses $p$ on the
right as a sentence whose truth varies across worlds. In the shallow
embedding, propositions are elements of an opaque type and have no
truth value of their own, and the earlier translation put
\lean{True} in place of $p$. A world then made every proposition true,
$\neg p$ included, and the axiom that the actual world is a world
entailed that there are no propositions at all.

\paragraph{Variable capture in a modal operator.}
The axiom that ordinary objects necessarily encode no properties was
written as $\Box(\lambda w.\,\neg\exists F\,\mathsf{Enc}\,w\,F)\,x$,
binding the encoding subject to the index of evaluation rather than to
the object $x$. With a universal accessibility relation this says that
nothing encodes anything, which contradicts the comprehension principle
for abstract objects as soon as there is one ordinary object and one
property.

\paragraph{The repair.}
The first two failures share a cause: one relation was asked to do the
work of two. Separating situated truth from obtaining, and placing the
classical clauses on obtaining, removes both. The four clauses are not
new content. They are the old clauses for situated truth, relocated,
and the classical behaviour of situated truth at worlds becomes a
theorem. Together with the corrected scope of the third axiom, and a
restriction of the closure of situations under parthood to abstract
parts, the repair reduced the number of axioms from 54 to 48, eight
former axioms becoming theorems. It also made room for the actuality
operator. In the earlier versions $\Obt$ did not exist, and an operator
defined from truth at \lean{actualWorld} would have been trivial,
since every index agreed with the actual world on every proposition.

\paragraph{The lesson.}
A shallow embedding inherits the meta-theory's logic, and a translation
error can make a theory degenerate while every proof still checks.
A model of the axioms, kept in step with them mechanically, is a cheap
and effective guard. Any one of the three failures would have blocked
the construction of a non-degenerate model, and keeping the model in
step means that the next such failure will be caught when it is
introduced.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

The logic of actuality and the two notions of validity are due to
Crossley and Humberstone~\cite{crossley1977} and Davies and
Humberstone~\cite{davies1980}. Kaplan's logic of
demonstratives~\cite{kaplan1989} exhibits related phenomena, such as
sentences true in every context that express contingent propositions.
Zalta's argument~\cite{zalta1988} and the ensuing
debate~\cite{hanson2006,nelson2012} concern which notion of validity
explicates logical truth; our formalization takes no side in that
debate but makes precise what each side is committed to.

Computational metaphysics in Zalta's tradition began with the use of
Prover9 to verify theorems of situation and world
theory~\cite{fitelson2007}. Kirchner, Benzm\"uller and Zalta embedded
the second-order fragment of Zalta's \emph{Principia
Logico-Metaphysica}, which includes an actuality operator, in
Isabelle/HOL using the method of shallow semantical
embeddings~\cite{benzmuller2013,kirchner2019,kirchner2020}. That
embedding distinguishes modally strict theorems, to which necessitation
applies, from theorems whose proofs depend on actuality axioms, and its
construction led to the discovery and repair of a paradox in the
theory. Our work is complementary. It is carried out in Lean~4 rather
than Isabelle/HOL. It states the real-world/general distinction as a
metatheorem about arbitrary classes of models, rather than building it
into a proof system. And it connects that metatheorem to a situation
theory for which a non-degenerate model is given and kept in step with
the axioms. To our knowledge, this is the first Lean formalization of
the real-world/general validity distinction and of contingent logical
truths.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have machine-checked the formal core of Zalta's claim that some
logical truths are not necessary. For every class of models, general
validity is closed under necessitation and real-world validity is not.
The actuality truth $\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi$ is
real-world valid, and its necessitation fails exactly where $\varphi$
is contingent. The same holds for an actuality operator defined inside
a situation theory, which is a model of the logic and has itself a
non-degenerate model.

Several directions remain. The propositional identities among the
axioms make propositions coarse-grained, which is at odds with the
hyperintensional propositions of Zalta's theory; removing them, and
giving a model with distinct but equivalent propositions, would bring
the development closer to its source. The link between the model and
the axioms is currently checked by a script comparing statements;
bundling the axioms into a Lean structure would let the kernel check it
directly. Defining situations as Zalta does, as abstract objects
encoding only propositional properties, would allow the actual world to
be obtained by comprehension and remove the remaining axiom about it.
Finally, the remaining basic theorems of situation and world
theory~\cite{zalta1993} are natural targets.
