\title[Contingent logical truths and real-world validity]{Contingent
logical truths and real-world validity,\\ with an application to
situation theory}

\author{Haniel Ulises}

\date{}

\subjclass[2020]{Primary 03B45; Secondary 03A05, 03B35}

\keywords{actuality operator, real-world validity, general validity,
contingent logical truth, necessitation, situation theory, abstract
objects}

\begin{abstract}
In modal languages with an actuality operator $\Act$, the sentence
$\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi$ is true at the actual world of
every model but is not, in general, necessary. Whether this exhibits a
logical truth that is not necessary depends on whether logical truth is
identified with real-world validity or with general validity. We give a
uniform treatment of the two notions relative to an arbitrary class of
Kripke models with a designated world. General validity is closed under
necessitation and real-world validity is not; the two notions separate
on every class containing a two-world model; and
$\Box(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)$ holds at a world exactly
when $\varphi$ is constant on the worlds accessible from it. We then
apply these results to an axiomatic theory of situations and worlds in
the tradition of Zalta, in which the actuality operator is definable
and the worlds are exactly the maximal possible situations. The theory,
read as a model of the logic of actuality, validates every real-world
valid formula at its actual world, and it has a non-degenerate model in
which the actuality biconditional for a contingent proposition is true
but not necessary. We also show that the theory must distinguish truth
in a situation from truth at an index: if the two are identified, there
is at most one situation. All results have been verified in an
interactive proof assistant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The rule of necessitation, from $\varphi$ infer $\Box\varphi$, is
admissible in every normal modal logic, and it reflects the traditional
view that whatever is logically true is necessarily true. Zalta
\cite{zalta1988} argued that this view fails for modal languages
containing a device that refers rigidly to the actual world. If $\Act$
is an actuality operator, so that $\Act\varphi$ is true at any world
just in case $\varphi$ is true at the actual world, then
$\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi$ is true at the actual world of
every interpretation. Yet if $\varphi$ is true but could have been
false, the biconditional fails at any world where $\varphi$ is false,
since $\Act\varphi$ remains true there.

The semantics of the actuality operator is due to Crossley and
Humberstone \cite{crossley1977}. Davies and Humberstone
\cite{davies1980} distinguished two notions of validity for it:
\emph{real-world validity}, truth at the designated world of every
model, and \emph{general validity}, truth at every world of every
model. Zalta's thesis is that real-world validity is the correct
explication of logical truth, and hence that some logical truths are
not necessary. Hanson \cite{hanson2006} defended the traditional
connection between logical truth and necessity, and Nelson and Zalta
\cite{nelson2012} replied.

The facts about the two notions of validity on which this debate turns
are elementary and largely known. Our first aim is to state them
uniformly for an arbitrary class of models, and to sharpen them in two
respects. First, the failure of necessitation for the actuality
biconditional admits an exact characterization:
$\Box(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)$ holds at a world precisely
when $\varphi$ is constant on the worlds accessible from it
(Theorem~\ref{thm:characterization}). Second, prefixing $\Act$ converts
real-world validity into general validity, so that the necessary truth
in the vicinity is $\Act(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)$ rather
than $\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:actually-necessary}).

Our second aim is to show that the phenomenon arises within a
substantive metaphysical theory, and not merely in arbitrary Kripke
models. We consider an axiomatic theory of situations and worlds in the
tradition of Zalta's theory of abstract objects
\cite{zalta1983,zalta1993}. In this theory the actuality operator is
definable without further axioms (Section~\ref{sec:theory}). Read as a
model of the logic of actuality, the theory validates every real-world
valid formula at its actual world (Theorem~\ref{thm:agreement}), and it
has a non-degenerate model in which the actuality biconditional for a
contingent proposition is true but not necessary
(Section~\ref{sec:model}). The theory distinguishes truth in a
situation, which is partial, from truth at an index, which is
classical. This distinction cannot be dropped: if truth in a situation
is required to be classical, the theory admits at most one situation
(Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}).

Section~\ref{sec:logic} introduces the logic of actuality and the two
notions of validity, and Section~\ref{sec:results} establishes the
results on validity and necessitation. Section~\ref{sec:theory}
presents the theory of situations and worlds and the actuality operator
within it, and Section~\ref{sec:model} constructs a model of the
theory. Section~\ref{sec:related} discusses related work.
Appendix~\ref{app:mech} describes the mechanical verification of the
results.

\section{The logic of actuality}
\label{sec:logic}

\begin{definition}
Let $\Phi$ be a set of propositional atoms. The language $\Lang(\Phi)$
is given by the grammar
\[
  \varphi \Coloneqq a \mid \bot \mid \neg\varphi \mid
  \varphi \wedge \varphi \mid \varphi \to \varphi \mid
  \Box\varphi \mid \Act\varphi
  \qquad (a \in \Phi).
\]
We write $\varphi \leftrightarrow \psi$ for
$(\varphi \to \psi) \wedge (\psi \to \varphi)$ and $\Diamond\varphi$
for $\neg\Box\neg\varphi$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
A \emph{model} is a tuple $\Mod = \langle W, R, \at, V\rangle$, where
$W$ is a nonempty set of worlds, $R \subseteq W \times W$ is an
accessibility relation, $\at \in W$ is the \emph{actual world}, and
$V\colon \Phi \to \mathcal{P}(W)$ is a valuation. Truth at a world is
defined by
\begin{align*}
  \Mod, w &\models a &&\text{iff}\quad w \in V(a),\\
  \Mod, w &\not\models \bot,\\
  \Mod, w &\models \neg\varphi &&\text{iff}\quad \Mod, w \not\models \varphi,\\
  \Mod, w &\models \varphi \wedge \psi &&\text{iff}\quad
     \Mod, w \models \varphi \text{ and } \Mod, w \models \psi,\\
  \Mod, w &\models \varphi \to \psi &&\text{iff}\quad
     \Mod, w \not\models \varphi \text{ or } \Mod, w \models \psi,\\
  \Mod, w &\models \Box\varphi &&\text{iff}\quad
     \Mod, v \models \varphi \text{ for all } v \text{ with } wRv,\\
  \Mod, w &\models \Act\varphi &&\text{iff}\quad \Mod, \at \models \varphi.
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

The truth condition of $\Act\varphi$ does not depend on the world of
evaluation.

\begin{lemma}[rigidity]
\label{lem:rigid}
For every model $\Mod$, formula $\varphi$ and worlds $w, w'$ of $\Mod$,
\[
  \Mod, w \models \Act\varphi
  \iff \Mod, w' \models \Act\varphi
  \iff \Mod, \at \models \varphi .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from the truth clause for $\Act$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
A \emph{class of models} is any collection $\Kl$ of models. We write
$\Kl_{\mathrm{all}}$ for the class of all models, $\Kl_{\mathrm{S5}}$
for the class of models in which $R$ is an equivalence relation, and
$\Kl_{\mathrm{u}}$ for the class of models in which $R = W \times W$.
A formula $\varphi$ is
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{real-world valid} in $\Kl$, written $\RWv{\Kl} \varphi$,
  if $\Mod, \at \models \varphi$ for every $\Mod \in \Kl$;
\item \emph{generally valid} in $\Kl$, written $\Gv{\Kl} \varphi$, if
  $\Mod, w \models \varphi$ for every $\Mod \in \Kl$ and every world
  $w$ of $\Mod$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
Let $\Two = \langle \{0,1\}, \{0,1\}^2, 1, V_2\rangle$, where
$V_2(a) = \{1\}$ for every $a \in \Phi$. Thus $\Two$ has two mutually
accessible worlds, the actual world $1$ and the world $0$, and every
atom is true at the actual world only. Clearly
$\Two \in \Kl_{\mathrm{u}} \subseteq \Kl_{\mathrm{S5}} \subseteq
\Kl_{\mathrm{all}}$.
\end{definition}

\section{Validity, necessitation and the actuality biconditional}
\label{sec:results}

Throughout this section, $\Kl$ is an arbitrary class of models.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:closure}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If $\Gv{\Kl} \varphi$, then $\RWv{\Kl} \varphi$.
\item If $\RWv{\Kl} \varphi \to \psi$ and $\RWv{\Kl} \varphi$, then
  $\RWv{\Kl} \psi$. The same holds for general validity.
\item If $\Gv{\Kl} \varphi$, then $\Gv{\Kl} \Box\varphi$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) The actual world of a model is one of its worlds. (ii) Modus ponens
preserves truth at each world, in particular at the actual world.
(iii) If $\varphi$ is true at every world of every model in $\Kl$, then
for each such model and each world $w$, $\varphi$ is true at every $v$
with $wRv$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:rw}
For every formula $\varphi$,
$\RWv{\Kl} \varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Mod \in \Kl$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid},
$\Mod, \at \models \Act\varphi$ if and only if
$\Mod, \at \models \varphi$, so the biconditional is true at $\at$.
\end{proof}

The necessitation of the actuality biconditional is governed by the
following characterization.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:characterization}
For every model $\Mod$, formula $\varphi$ and world $w$ of $\Mod$,
\[
  \Mod, w \models \Box(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)
  \iff
  \text{for every } v \text{ with } wRv,\quad
  \Mod, v \models \varphi \iff \Mod, \at \models \varphi .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the truth clauses for $\Box$ and $\leftrightarrow$, the left-hand
side holds if and only if, for every $v$ with $wRv$,
$\Mod, v \models \varphi$ iff $\Mod, v \models \Act\varphi$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid}, the latter is equivalent to
$\Mod, \at \models \varphi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:universal}
Let $\Mod \in \Kl_{\mathrm{u}}$. For every world $w$ of $\Mod$,
$\Mod, w \models \Box(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)$ if and only
if $\varphi$ is true at every world of $\Mod$ or false at every world
of $\Mod$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $\Mod, w \models \Box(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)$.
Since $R$ is universal, Theorem~\ref{thm:characterization} gives that
the truth value of $\varphi$ at every world equals its truth value at
$\at$. Conversely, if $\varphi$ has the same truth value at every
world, then in particular its value at each $v$ agrees with its value
at $\at$.
\end{proof}

In universal models, therefore, the necessitation of the actuality
biconditional fails exactly when $\varphi$ is contingent. A single
contingent atom suffices to separate the two notions of validity.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:separation}
Suppose that $\Two \in \Kl$, and let $a \in \Phi$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\nGv{\Kl} a \leftrightarrow \Act a$;
\item $\nRWv{\Kl} \Box(a \leftrightarrow \Act a)$;
\item real-world validity in $\Kl$ is not closed under necessitation;
\item real-world validity in $\Kl$ properly extends general validity
  in $\Kl$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, \textup{(i)--(iv)} hold for $\Kl_{\mathrm{all}}$,
$\Kl_{\mathrm{S5}}$ and $\Kl_{\mathrm{u}}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In $\Two$ we have $\Two, 0 \not\models a$, while
$\Two, 0 \models \Act a$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid}, since
$\Two, 1 \models a$. Hence $a \leftrightarrow \Act a$ fails at $0$,
which gives (i). Since $1 R 0$, the formula
$\Box(a \leftrightarrow \Act a)$ fails at the actual world $1$, which
gives (ii). By Theorem~\ref{thm:rw}, $a \leftrightarrow \Act a$ is
real-world valid in $\Kl$; hence (ii) yields (iii), and (i) together
with Proposition~\ref{prop:closure}(i) yields (iv).
\end{proof}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:closure}(iii), the failure of necessitation is
specific to real-world validity. If logical truth is identified with
real-world validity, then $a \leftrightarrow \Act a$ is a logical truth
that is not necessary. If it is identified with general validity, then
$a \leftrightarrow \Act a$ is not a logical truth.

The actuality operator interacts differently with the two notions.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:inert}
For every formula $\varphi$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\RWv{\Kl} \Act\varphi$ if and only if $\RWv{\Kl} \varphi$;
\item $\Gv{\Kl} \Act\varphi$ if and only if $\RWv{\Kl} \varphi$;
\item $\Gv{\Kl_{\mathrm{all}}} \Act\varphi \to \Box\Act\varphi$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid}, the truth value of $\Act\varphi$ at any world
of $\Mod$ is the truth value of $\varphi$ at $\at$; this gives (i) and
(ii). For (iii), if $\Act\varphi$ is true at $w$, then $\varphi$ is
true at $\at$, so $\Act\varphi$ is true at every world, and in
particular at every $v$ with $wRv$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:actually-necessary}
For every formula $\varphi$,
$\Gv{\Kl} \Act(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)$ and
$\Gv{\Kl} \Box\Act(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first claim follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:rw} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:inert}(ii), and the second from the first by
Proposition~\ref{prop:closure}(iii).
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:actually-necessary} identifies the necessary truth
associated with the actuality biconditional. It is not necessary that
$\varphi$ holds if and only if $\varphi$ actually holds; it is
necessary, and generally valid, that this biconditional actually holds.

\section{Actuality in a theory of situations and worlds}
\label{sec:theory}

We now consider a theory $\T$ of situations and worlds in the tradition
of Zalta's theory of abstract objects \cite{zalta1983,zalta1988book}
and his development of situation and world theory \cite{zalta1993}. The
theory is formulated in the metalanguage of higher-order logic.

\subsection{Signature}

The theory has three sorts: a domain $\Dom$ of \emph{objects}, which
also serve as indices of modal evaluation; a sort $\Prop$ of
\emph{propositions}; and a sort $\Pty$ of \emph{properties}. Its
primitive vocabulary consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item operations $\pneg\colon \Prop \to \Prop$ and
  $\pand, \por, \pimp, \piff\colon \Prop \times \Prop \to \Prop$;
\item a map $\VAC\colon \Prop \to \Pty$, sending $p$ to the property
  of being such that $p$;
\item predicates $\Obj, \Sit \subseteq \Dom$ (being an object, being a
  situation) and a relation $\Conc \subseteq \Dom \times \Dom$, where
  $\Conc(w,x)$ expresses that $x$ is concrete at the index $w$;
\item encoding relations $\Enc \subseteq \Dom \times \Pty$ and
  $\Encp \subseteq \Dom \times \Prop$;
\item \emph{truth in a situation}, $\models\ \subseteq \Dom \times
  \Prop$, written $s \models p$;
\item \emph{obtaining at an index}, $\Obt \subseteq \Dom \times
  \Prop$, where $\Obt(v,p)$ expresses that $p$ is the case at $v$;
\item a constant $\at \in \Dom$, the actual world;
\item operations $\meet, \join\colon \Dom \times \Dom \to \Dom$.
\end{enumerate}

The modal operators act on conditions on indices. Accessibility is
universal: for $P \subseteq \Dom$, $(\Box P)(w)$ holds if and only if
$P(v)$ for every $v \in \Dom$, and $\Diamond P = \neg\Box\neg P$. The
operators $\Box$ and $\Diamond$ thus satisfy S5 without further axioms.
We use the following abbreviations:
\begin{align*}
  s \partof s' &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\; \forall F\,
    (\Enc(s,F) \to \Enc(s',F)) &&\text{(parthood)}\\
  \Ord(x) &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\;
    \Diamond(\lambda w.\,\Conc(w,x))(x) &&\text{(ordinary object)}\\
  \Abs(x) &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\; \neg\Ord(x)
    &&\text{(abstract object)}\\
  \Max(s) &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\; \forall p\,
    (s \models p \;\vee\; s \models \pneg p) &&\text{(maximality)}\\
  \Cons(s) &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\; \neg\exists p\,
    (s \models p \;\wedge\; s \models \pneg p) &&\text{(consistency)}
\end{align*}

\subsection{Axioms}

The axioms of $\T$ fall into seven groups. Free variables are
universally quantified.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(T\arabic*), leftmargin=3.2em]
\item \label{ax:boolean} \emph{Propositional identities.} The
  operations on $\Prop$ satisfy double negation, the De Morgan laws,
  commutativity and associativity of $\pand$ and $\por$, and the
  identities $p \pimp q = \pneg p \por q$ and
  $p \piff q = (p \pimp q) \pand (q \pimp p)$.
\item \label{ax:encoding} \emph{Encoding and truth.} If $\Obj(x)$, then
  $\Encp(x,p) \leftrightarrow \Enc(x, \VAC(p))$ and
  $x \models p \leftrightarrow \Encp(x,p)$. Every situation is an
  object, and every property encoded by a situation is of the form
  $\VAC(p)$.
\item \label{ax:obtains} \emph{Obtaining.} For every index $v$,
  \begin{align*}
    \Obt(v, \pneg p) &\leftrightarrow \neg\Obt(v,p), &
    \Obt(v, p \pand q) &\leftrightarrow \Obt(v,p) \wedge \Obt(v,q),\\
    \Obt(v, p \por q) &\leftrightarrow \Obt(v,p) \vee \Obt(v,q), &
    \Obt(v, p \pimp q) &\leftrightarrow (\Obt(v,p) \to \Obt(v,q)).
  \end{align*}
\item \label{ax:abstract} \emph{Abstract objects.} Ordinary objects
  necessarily encode no property:
  $\Ord(x) \to \Box(\lambda w.\,\neg\exists F\,\Enc(x,F))(x)$. For
  every condition $\varphi$ on properties there is an abstract object
  encoding exactly the properties that satisfy it:
  $\exists x\,(\Abs(x) \wedge \forall F\,(\Enc(x,F) \leftrightarrow
  \varphi(F)))$. Abstract objects that encode the same properties are
  identical.
\item \label{ax:ext} \emph{Situations.} Situations that make the same
  propositions true are identical. If $\Sit(s)$, $\Abs(x)$ and
  $x \partof s$, then $\Sit(x)$.
\item \label{ax:lattice} \emph{Meet and join.} If $\Sit(s)$ and
  $\Sit(s')$, then $\Sit(s \meet s')$ and $\Sit(s \join s')$. For all
  $s, s', u$: $(s \meet s') \models p \leftrightarrow (s \models p
  \wedge s' \models p)$; $s \partof s \join s'$ and $s' \partof s \join s'$;
  and if $s \partof u$ and $s' \partof u$, then $s \join s' \partof u$.
\item \label{ax:actual} \emph{The actual world.} $\Sit(\at)$, and
  $\at \models p \leftrightarrow \Obt(\at, p)$ for every $p$.
\end{enumerate}

Axiom group~\ref{ax:obtains} imposes the classical truth conditions on
obtaining and not on truth in a situation, so that the latter may be
partial; Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} shows that this is
unavoidable. Axiom group~\ref{ax:actual} relates the two roles of
$\at$, as a situation and as the index of evaluation.

\subsection{Worlds}

Following Zalta, a world is a situation that could make true exactly
what is the case, and a situation is actual if everything true in it is
the case. Since the propositions of $\T$ do not themselves carry truth
values, ``$p$ is the case'' is expressed by obtaining at the index of
evaluation:
\begin{align*}
  \World(s) &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\; \Sit(s) \wedge
    \Diamond\bigl(\lambda v.\,\forall p\,
      (s \models p \leftrightarrow \Obt(v,p))\bigr)(\at),\\
  \Actual(s) &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\; \forall p\,
    (s \models p \to \Obt(\at, p)),\\
  \Poss(s) &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\;
    \Diamond\bigl(\lambda v.\,\forall p\,
      (s \models p \to \Obt(v,p))\bigr)(\at).
\end{align*}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:worlds}
The following are theorems of $\T$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If $\World(w)$, there is an index $v$ such that
  $w \models p \leftrightarrow \Obt(v,p)$ for every $p$.
\item If $\World(w)$, then $w \models \pneg p \leftrightarrow
  \neg(w \models p)$, and truth at $w$ satisfies the classical clauses
  for $\pand$, $\por$ and $\pimp$.
\item If $\World(w)$, then $\Max(w)$, $\Cons(w)$ and $\Poss(w)$.
\item If $\Poss(s)$, then $\Cons(s)$.
\item $\World(s) \leftrightarrow \Sit(s) \wedge \Max(s) \wedge \Poss(s)$.
\item $\World(\at)$ and $\Actual(\at)$. If $\Actual(s)$, then
  $\Poss(s)$ and $\Cons(s)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) Since accessibility is universal, $(\Diamond P)(\at)$ is equivalent
to $\exists v\, P(v)$.
(ii) Let $v$ be as in (i); the clauses transfer from~\ref{ax:obtains}.
(iii) Maximality and consistency follow from the clause for $\pneg$ in
(ii), and the index $v$ of (i) witnesses $\Poss(w)$.
(iv) Let $v$ witness $\Poss(s)$. If $s \models p$ and
$s \models \pneg p$, then $\Obt(v,p)$ and $\Obt(v,\pneg p)$, contrary
to~\ref{ax:obtains}.
(v) The forward direction is (iii). Conversely, let $v$ witness
$\Poss(s)$; it suffices to show that $\Obt(v,p)$ implies
$s \models p$. By maximality, $s \models p$ or $s \models \pneg p$, and
the second alternative yields $\Obt(v,\pneg p)$, which is incompatible
with $\Obt(v,p)$.
(vi) By~\ref{ax:actual}, the index $\at$ witnesses $\World(\at)$, and
$\Actual(\at)$ is one direction of~\ref{ax:actual}. If $\Actual(s)$,
then $\at$ witnesses $\Poss(s)$, and $\Cons(s)$ follows by (iv).
\end{proof}

By part~(v), the worlds are exactly the maximal possible situations.
Consistency need not be required, since by part~(iv) it follows from
possibility.

\subsection{Truth in a situation and truth at an index}

The distinction between truth in a situation and obtaining at an index
is essential to $\T$. If truth in a situation is required to satisfy
the classical clause for negation, the theory collapses.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:collapse}
Let $\T^-$ be the extension of $\T$ by the schema
\[
  s \models \pneg p \;\leftrightarrow\; \neg(s \models p)
  \qquad (s \in \Dom,\ p \in \Prop).
\]
In $\T^-$, any two objects make the same propositions true, and there
is at most one situation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $s, s' \in \Dom$ and $p \in \Prop$. By the schema and the meet
axiom in~\ref{ax:lattice}, the truth of $\pneg p$ in $s \meet s'$ can
be computed in two ways:
\[
  \neg\bigl(s \models p \wedge s' \models p\bigr)
  \;\leftrightarrow\; (s \meet s') \models \pneg p
  \;\leftrightarrow\; \neg(s \models p) \wedge \neg(s' \models p).
\]
If exactly one of $s \models p$ and $s' \models p$ holds, the left-hand
side is true and the right-hand side is false. Hence
$s \models p \leftrightarrow s' \models p$. By the extensionality
axiom in~\ref{ax:ext}, any two situations are identical.
\end{proof}

The schema of Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} also forces every
situation to be maximal, so that no situation could be partial. A
related difficulty arises if ``$p$ is the case'' in the definition of
worldhood is replaced by a tautology.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:empty}
Let $\World^\top(s)$ abbreviate $\Sit(s) \wedge
\Diamond(\lambda v.\,\forall p\,(s \models p \leftrightarrow
\top))(\at)$. If $\World^\top(\at)$ and
$\at \models \pneg p \leftrightarrow \neg(\at \models p)$ for every
$p$, then $\Prop$ is empty.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The assumption $\World^\top(\at)$ gives $\at \models p$ for every $p$,
and in particular $\at \models p$ and $\at \models \pneg p$, which
together with the clause for negation is a contradiction for any
$p \in \Prop$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The actuality operator}

In $\T$ the actuality operator is definable. For a condition
$P \subseteq \Dom$ on indices, let
\[
  (\Act P)(w) \;:\Longleftrightarrow\; P(\at).
\]
The operator $\Act$ is rigid and idempotent, and
$\Box\Act P \leftrightarrow \Act P \leftrightarrow \Diamond\Act P$
holds at every index. The actuality of a situation is the
actualization of its possibility condition: for every index $w$,
$\Actual(s)$ holds if and only if
$\Act\bigl(\lambda v.\,\forall p\,(s \models p \to \Obt(v,p))\bigr)(w)$.

A condition $P$ is \emph{necessary} at $w$ if $(\Box P)(w)$,
\emph{impossible} at $w$ if $(\Box\neg P)(w)$, and \emph{contingent} at
$w$ if $(\Diamond P)(w)$ and $(\Diamond\neg P)(w)$.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:theory-actuality}
In $\T$, for every condition $P$ and index $w$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $P(\at) \leftrightarrow (\Act P)(\at)$;
\item $\Box(\lambda v.\, P(v) \leftrightarrow (\Act P)(v))(w)$ holds if
  and only if $P$ is necessary or impossible at $w$;
\item if $P$ is contingent at $w$, then
  $\Box(\lambda v.\, P(v) \leftrightarrow (\Act P)(v))(w)$ fails.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, for every proposition $q$ whose obtaining is contingent
at $\at$, the condition $\Obt(\at,q) \leftrightarrow
\Act(\lambda v.\,\Obt(v,q))(\at)$ holds but is not necessary.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Part (i) is the definition of $\Act$. For (ii), the left-hand side
states that $P(v) \leftrightarrow P(\at)$ for every $v$. If $P(\at)$,
then $P$ is necessary at $w$; otherwise $P$ is impossible at $w$.
Conversely, a necessary or impossible condition is constant on $\Dom$
and therefore agrees everywhere with its value at $\at$. Part (iii)
follows from (ii), since a contingent condition is neither necessary
nor impossible.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The theory as a model of the logic of actuality}

The theory determines a model of the logic of
Section~\ref{sec:logic}, with the propositions of $\T$ as atoms.

\begin{definition}
Let $\Mod_\T = \langle \Dom, \Dom \times \Dom, \at, V_\T\rangle$, with
set of atoms $\Prop$ and $V_\T(q) = \{v \in \Dom : \Obt(v,q)\}$. The
translation $\tr{\cdot}$ from $\Lang(\Prop)$ to conditions on $\Dom$ is
defined by $\tr{q} = \lambda v.\,\Obt(v,q)$, by commutation with the
Boolean connectives, and by $\tr{\Box\varphi} = \Box\tr{\varphi}$ and
$\tr{\Act\varphi} = \Act\tr{\varphi}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:agreement}
For every $\varphi \in \Lang(\Prop)$ and $v \in \Dom$,
$\Mod_\T, v \models \varphi$ if and only if $\tr{\varphi}(v)$.
Consequently, if $\RWv{\Kl_{\mathrm{u}}} \varphi$, then
$\tr{\varphi}(\at)$ is a theorem of $\T$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By induction on $\varphi$. The atomic case is the definition of
$V_\T$, and the Boolean cases are immediate. In the case of
$\Box\varphi$, both sides quantify over all of $\Dom$, and in the case
of $\Act\varphi$, both sides reduce to the induction hypothesis at
$\at$. For the second claim, $\Mod_\T \in \Kl_{\mathrm{u}}$, so
$\Mod_\T, \at \models \varphi$.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:agreement} shows that the operator defined in $\T$ is
the actuality operator of Crossley and Humberstone, and that every
formula real-world valid over universal models holds at the actual
world of $\T$. The theory does not determine whether any proposition is
contingent; this is settled in the model constructed in the next
section.

\section{A model of the theory}
\label{sec:model}

We construct a model $\N$ of $\T$. Let $\mathbb{B} = \{0,1\}$ be a set
of two valuations, with $1$ the actual valuation, and for
$b \in \mathbb{B}$ let $S_b = \{p \subseteq \mathbb{B} : b \in p\}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Prop = \Pty = \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B})$, the propositional
  operations are the Boolean operations, and $\VAC$ is the identity.
\item $\Dom = \{\iota_b : b \in \mathbb{B}\} \sqcup
  \{\alpha_S : S \subseteq \Pty\}$. The elements $\iota_b$ are the
  \emph{indices} and the elements $\alpha_S$ are the \emph{abstract
  objects}.
\item $\Conc(w,x)$ holds if and only if $x$ is an index.
  $\Enc(\iota_b, F)$ never holds, $\Enc(\alpha_S, F)$ holds if and only
  if $F \in S$, and $\Encp(x,p)$ holds if and only if $\Enc(x,p)$.
\item $\Obj = \Dom$, and $\Sit(x)$ holds if and only if $x$ is an
  abstract object.
\item $x \models p$ if and only if $\Encp(x,p)$; thus
  $\alpha_S \models p$ if and only if $p \in S$.
\item $\Obt(\iota_b, p)$ if and only if $b \in p$, and
  $\Obt(\alpha_S, p)$ if and only if $1 \in p$.
\item $\at = \alpha_{S_1}$.
\item $x \meet y = \alpha_{\{F : \Enc(x,F) \wedge \Enc(y,F)\}}$ and
  $x \join y = \alpha_{\{F : \Enc(x,F) \vee \Enc(y,F)\}}$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:model}
$\N$ satisfies every axiom of $\T$. Consequently, $\T$ is consistent
relative to higher-order logic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We verify the axiom groups in turn.
\ref{ax:boolean} holds because $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B})$ is a Boolean
algebra.
\ref{ax:encoding} holds by definition, since every property is of the
form $\VAC(p)$.
\ref{ax:obtains} holds because, for fixed $v$, $\Obt(v,\cdot)$ is
membership of a fixed valuation.
For~\ref{ax:abstract}: every index is concrete at every index, hence
ordinary, and encodes no property; no $\alpha_S$ is concrete at any
index, so every $\alpha_S$ is abstract. Comprehension for a condition
$\varphi$ is witnessed by $\alpha_{\{F : \varphi(F)\}}$, and abstract
objects encoding the same properties have the same index set $S$ and
are therefore identical.
For~\ref{ax:ext}: the situations are the $\alpha_S$, and $\alpha_S$
makes true exactly the members of $S$, which gives extensionality; an
abstract part of a situation is some $\alpha_T$, hence a situation.
\ref{ax:lattice} holds because meet and join are intersection and union
of encoded properties; in particular the properties encoded by
$x \join y$ form the least set containing those encoded by $x$ and by
$y$.
\ref{ax:actual} holds since $\at \models p$ if and only if $1 \in p$, if
and only if $\Obt(\at, p)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:nondegenerate}
In $\N$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\alpha_{S_1}$ and $\alpha_{S_0}$ are distinct worlds;
\item the proposition $c = \{1\}$ is contingent: it obtains at
  $\iota_1$ and does not obtain at $\iota_0$;
\item the situation $\alpha_\varnothing$ is partial and possible but is
  not a world, and $\alpha_\varnothing \partof \at$ with
  $\alpha_\varnothing \neq \at$;
\item the situation $\alpha_{\Pty}$ is not possible;
\item there are both ordinary and abstract objects.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) The index $\iota_b$ witnesses $\World(\alpha_{S_b})$, and
$c \in S_1 \setminus S_0$. (ii) is immediate.
(iii) $\alpha_\varnothing$ makes neither $c$ nor $\pneg c$ true; it is
possible because it makes nothing true; it is not a world by
Theorem~\ref{thm:worlds}(iii), since it is not maximal; and it encodes
no property, so it is a part of every object.
(iv) $\alpha_{\Pty}$ makes both $c$ and $\pneg c$ true, so it is not
consistent, and hence not possible by Theorem~\ref{thm:worlds}(iv).
(v) The indices are ordinary and the $\alpha_S$ are abstract.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:contingent-truth}
In $\N$, the condition
$\Obt(\at,c) \leftrightarrow \Act(\lambda v.\,\Obt(v,c))(\at)$ holds,
but its necessitation fails: the proposition $c$ does not obtain at the
index $\iota_0$, although it actually obtains.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:theory-actuality} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:nondegenerate}(ii).
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:contingent-truth} exhibits the phenomenon of
Section~\ref{sec:results} in a model of the full theory, in which
situations may be partial, there is more than one world, and ordinary
and abstract objects coexist. By Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}, no
model of $\T^-$ has two distinct worlds.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

The actuality operator and its semantics are due to Crossley and
Humberstone \cite{crossley1977}. The distinction between real-world and
general validity, and the failure of necessitation for the former, are
due to Davies and Humberstone \cite{davies1980}, who also introduced the
operator ``fixedly''. Kaplan's logic of demonstratives
\cite{kaplan1989} exhibits a closely related phenomenon: sentences true
in every context of utterance that nonetheless express contingent
propositions. Zalta \cite{zalta1988} argued that real-world validity
explicates logical truth, so that some logical truths are not
necessary; Hanson \cite{hanson2006} defended general validity, and
Nelson and Zalta \cite{nelson2012} replied. The results of
Section~\ref{sec:results} are neutral between these positions. They
make precise what each position is committed to, and they identify the
necessary truth associated with the actuality biconditional
(Corollary~\ref{cor:actually-necessary}).

The theory of Section~\ref{sec:theory} follows Zalta's theory of
abstract objects \cite{zalta1983,zalta1988book} and his treatment of
situations and worlds \cite{zalta1993}. Fitelson and Zalta
\cite{fitelson2007} verified a number of theorems of situation and world
theory with an automated reasoning system. Kirchner, Benzm\"uller and
Zalta \cite{kirchner2019,kirchner2020} embedded the higher-order
fragment of Zalta's \emph{Principia Logico-Metaphysica}, which includes
an actuality operator, in Isabelle/HOL by the method of shallow
semantical embeddings \cite{benzmuller2013}. Their embedding
distinguishes modally strict theorems, to which necessitation applies,
from theorems that depend on the axioms of actuality, and its
construction led to the discovery and resolution of a paradox in the
theory. The present work differs in treating real-world and general
validity as properties of formulas relative to arbitrary classes of
models, in characterizing exactly when the actuality biconditional is
necessary, and in exhibiting the phenomenon in a model of a theory of
situations that separates truth in a situation from truth at an index.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For every class of models, general validity is closed under
necessitation and real-world validity is not, and the two notions
separate on every class containing a two-world model. The actuality
biconditional is real-world valid, its necessitation holds exactly
where its argument is constant, and its actualization is generally
valid. The same holds for the definable actuality operator of a theory
of situations and worlds. That theory is a model of the logic of
actuality, and it has a non-degenerate model in which the biconditional
for a contingent proposition is true but not necessary. The theory
requires truth in a situation to be distinguished from truth at an
index.

Three questions remain open. The identities~\ref{ax:boolean} make
propositions coarse-grained, whereas the propositions of Zalta's theory
are hyperintensional; a model of a variant of $\T$ with distinct but
necessarily equivalent propositions would bring the theory closer to
its source. If situations are defined, as in Zalta's theory, as
abstract objects encoding only propositional properties, the actual
world should be obtainable by comprehension and axiom
group~\ref{ax:actual} derivable. Finally, the extension of the results
of Section~\ref{sec:results} to quantified modal logic, where the
actuality operator interacts with variable domains, is of independent
interest.
