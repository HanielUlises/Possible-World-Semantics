\title[Contingent logical truths and real-world validity]{Contingent
logical truths and real-world validity,\\ with an application to
situation theory}

\author{Haniel Ulises}

\date{}

\subjclass[2020]{Primary 03B45; Secondary 03A05, 03B35}

\keywords{actuality operator, real-world validity, general validity,
contingent logical truth, necessitation, situation theory, abstract
objects}

\begin{abstract}
In modal languages with an actuality operator $\Act$, the sentence
$\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi$ is true at the actual world of
every model but is not, in general, necessary. Whether this exhibits a
logical truth that is not necessary depends on whether logical truth is
identified with real-world validity or with general validity. We give a
uniform treatment of the two notions relative to an arbitrary class of
Kripke models with a designated world. General validity is closed under
necessitation and real-world validity is not; the two notions separate
on every class containing a two-world model; and
$\Box(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)$ holds at a world exactly
when $\varphi$ is constant on the worlds accessible from it. The
results extend to quantified modal logic with variable domains, where
``everything actually exists'' is real-world valid but not necessary
even when every predicate is rigid. We then
apply these results to an axiomatic theory of situations and worlds in
the tradition of Zalta, in which the actuality operator is definable
and the worlds are exactly the maximal possible situations. The theory,
read as a model of the logic of actuality, validates every real-world
valid formula at its actual world, and it has a non-degenerate model in
which the actuality biconditional for a contingent proposition is true
but not necessary, and in which necessarily equivalent propositions may
be distinct. Since concreteness in the theory is relative to an index,
actualist quantification over the concrete objects is definable, and
the quantified contingent logical truths arise within the theory as
well: ``everything concrete is actually concrete'' holds at its actual
world, and in the model it is not necessary. We also show that the theory must distinguish truth
in a situation from truth at an index: if the two are identified, there
is at most one situation. All results have been verified in an
interactive proof assistant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The rule of necessitation, from $\varphi$ infer $\Box\varphi$, is
admissible in every normal modal logic, and it reflects the traditional
view that whatever is logically true is necessarily true. Zalta
\cite{zalta1988} argued that this view fails for modal languages
containing a device that refers rigidly to the actual world. If $\Act$
is an actuality operator, so that $\Act\varphi$ is true at any world
just in case $\varphi$ is true at the actual world, then
$\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi$ is true at the actual world of
every interpretation. Yet if $\varphi$ is true but could have been
false, the biconditional fails at any world where $\varphi$ is false,
since $\Act\varphi$ remains true there.

The semantics of the actuality operator is due to Crossley and
Humberstone \cite{crossley1977}. Davies and Humberstone
\cite{davies1980} distinguished two notions of validity for it:
\emph{real-world validity}, truth at the designated world of every
model, and \emph{general validity}, truth at every world of every
model. Zalta's thesis is that real-world validity is the correct
explication of logical truth, and hence that some logical truths are
not necessary. Hanson \cite{hanson2006} defended the traditional
connection between logical truth and necessity, and Nelson and Zalta
\cite{nelson2012} replied.

The facts about the two notions of validity on which this debate turns
are elementary and largely known. Our first aim is to state them
uniformly for an arbitrary class of models, and to sharpen them in two
respects. First, the failure of necessitation for the actuality
biconditional admits an exact characterization:
$\Box(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)$ holds at a world precisely
when $\varphi$ is constant on the worlds accessible from it
(Theorem~\ref{thm:characterization}). Second, prefixing $\Act$ converts
real-world validity into general validity, so that the necessary truth
in the vicinity is $\Act(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)$ rather
than $\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:actually-necessary}). The results extend to
first-order modal logic with variable domains, where the domains
themselves give rise to contingent logical truths, such as
``everything actually exists'' (Section~\ref{sec:quantified}).

Our second aim is to show that the phenomenon arises within a
substantive metaphysical theory, and not merely in arbitrary Kripke
models. We consider an axiomatic theory of situations and worlds in the
tradition of Zalta's theory of abstract objects
\cite{zalta1983,zalta1993}. As in Zalta's theory, situations are
abstract objects that encode only propositional properties, and the
actual world is obtained by comprehension
(Proposition~\ref{prop:situations}). The actuality operator is
definable without further axioms (Section~\ref{sec:theory}). Read as a
model of the logic of actuality, the theory validates every real-world
valid formula at its actual world (Theorem~\ref{thm:agreement}), and it
has a non-degenerate model in which the actuality biconditional for a
contingent proposition is true but not necessary
(Section~\ref{sec:model}). The quantifiers of the theory range over a
single domain, but concreteness is relative to an index, so the
concrete objects at an index form a domain that may vary. Actualist
quantification over them is definable, the theory is a model of
quantified modal logic with these domains
(Theorem~\ref{thm:q-agreement}), and in the model of the theory
``everything concrete is actually concrete'' is true but not necessary
(Corollary~\ref{cor:q-contingent-truth}). The theory distinguishes truth in a
situation, which is partial, from truth at an index, which is
classical. This distinction cannot be dropped: if truth in a situation
is required to be classical, the theory admits at most one situation
(Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}).

Section~\ref{sec:logic} introduces the logic of actuality and the two
notions of validity, Section~\ref{sec:results} establishes the
results on validity and necessitation, and Section~\ref{sec:quantified}
extends them to quantified modal logic. Section~\ref{sec:theory}
presents the theory of situations and worlds and the actuality operator
within it, and Section~\ref{sec:model} constructs a model of the
theory. Section~\ref{sec:related} discusses related work.
Appendix~\ref{app:mech} describes the mechanical verification of the
results.

\section{The logic of actuality}
\label{sec:logic}

\begin{definition}
Let $\Phi$ be a set of propositional atoms. The language $\Lang(\Phi)$
is given by the grammar
\[
  \varphi \Coloneqq a \mid \bot \mid \neg\varphi \mid
  \varphi \wedge \varphi \mid \varphi \to \varphi \mid
  \Box\varphi \mid \Act\varphi
  \qquad (a \in \Phi).
\]
We write $\varphi \leftrightarrow \psi$ for
$(\varphi \to \psi) \wedge (\psi \to \varphi)$ and $\Diamond\varphi$
for $\neg\Box\neg\varphi$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
A \emph{model} is a tuple $\Mod = \langle W, R, \at, V\rangle$, where
$W$ is a nonempty set of worlds, $R \subseteq W \times W$ is an
accessibility relation, $\at \in W$ is the \emph{actual world}, and
$V\colon \Phi \to \mathcal{P}(W)$ is a valuation. Truth at a world is
defined by
\begin{align*}
  \Mod, w &\models a &&\text{iff}\quad w \in V(a),\\
  \Mod, w &\not\models \bot,\\
  \Mod, w &\models \neg\varphi &&\text{iff}\quad \Mod, w \not\models \varphi,\\
  \Mod, w &\models \varphi \wedge \psi &&\text{iff}\quad
     \Mod, w \models \varphi \text{ and } \Mod, w \models \psi,\\
  \Mod, w &\models \varphi \to \psi &&\text{iff}\quad
     \Mod, w \not\models \varphi \text{ or } \Mod, w \models \psi,\\
  \Mod, w &\models \Box\varphi &&\text{iff}\quad
     \Mod, v \models \varphi \text{ for all } v \text{ with } wRv,\\
  \Mod, w &\models \Act\varphi &&\text{iff}\quad \Mod, \at \models \varphi.
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

The truth condition of $\Act\varphi$ does not depend on the world of
evaluation.

\begin{lemma}[rigidity]
\label{lem:rigid}
For every model $\Mod$, formula $\varphi$ and worlds $w, w'$ of $\Mod$,
\[
  \Mod, w \models \Act\varphi
  \iff \Mod, w' \models \Act\varphi
  \iff \Mod, \at \models \varphi .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from the truth clause for $\Act$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
A \emph{class of models} is any collection $\Kl$ of models. We write
$\Kl_{\mathrm{all}}$ for the class of all models, $\Kl_{\mathrm{S5}}$
for the class of models in which $R$ is an equivalence relation, and
$\Kl_{\mathrm{u}}$ for the class of models in which $R = W \times W$.
A formula $\varphi$ is
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{real-world valid} in $\Kl$, written $\RWv{\Kl} \varphi$,
  if $\Mod, \at \models \varphi$ for every $\Mod \in \Kl$;
\item \emph{generally valid} in $\Kl$, written $\Gv{\Kl} \varphi$, if
  $\Mod, w \models \varphi$ for every $\Mod \in \Kl$ and every world
  $w$ of $\Mod$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
Let $\Two = \langle \{0,1\}, \{0,1\}^2, 1, V_2\rangle$, where
$V_2(a) = \{1\}$ for every $a \in \Phi$. Thus $\Two$ has two mutually
accessible worlds, the actual world $1$ and the world $0$, and every
atom is true at the actual world only. Clearly
$\Two \in \Kl_{\mathrm{u}} \subseteq \Kl_{\mathrm{S5}} \subseteq
\Kl_{\mathrm{all}}$; see Figure~\ref{fig:two}.
\end{definition}

\section{Validity, necessitation and the actuality biconditional}
\label{sec:results}

Throughout this section, $\Kl$ is an arbitrary class of models.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:closure}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If $\Gv{\Kl} \varphi$, then $\RWv{\Kl} \varphi$.
\item If $\RWv{\Kl} \varphi \to \psi$ and $\RWv{\Kl} \varphi$, then
  $\RWv{\Kl} \psi$. The same holds for general validity.
\item If $\Gv{\Kl} \varphi$, then $\Gv{\Kl} \Box\varphi$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) The actual world of a model is one of its worlds. (ii) Modus ponens
preserves truth at each world, in particular at the actual world.
(iii) If $\varphi$ is true at every world of every model in $\Kl$, then
for each such model and each world $w$, $\varphi$ is true at every $v$
with $wRv$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:rw}
For every formula $\varphi$,
$\RWv{\Kl} \varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Mod \in \Kl$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid},
$\Mod, \at \models \Act\varphi$ if and only if
$\Mod, \at \models \varphi$, so the biconditional is true at $\at$.
\end{proof}

The necessitation of the actuality biconditional is governed by the
following characterization.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:characterization}
For every model $\Mod$, formula $\varphi$ and world $w$ of $\Mod$,
\[
  \Mod, w \models \Box(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)
  \iff
  \text{for every } v \text{ with } wRv,\quad
  \Mod, v \models \varphi \iff \Mod, \at \models \varphi .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the truth clauses for $\Box$ and $\leftrightarrow$, the left-hand
side holds if and only if, for every $v$ with $wRv$,
$\Mod, v \models \varphi$ iff $\Mod, v \models \Act\varphi$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid}, the latter is equivalent to
$\Mod, \at \models \varphi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:universal}
Let $\Mod \in \Kl_{\mathrm{u}}$. For every world $w$ of $\Mod$,
$\Mod, w \models \Box(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)$ if and only
if $\varphi$ is true at every world of $\Mod$ or false at every world
of $\Mod$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $\Mod, w \models \Box(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)$.
Since $R$ is universal, Theorem~\ref{thm:characterization} gives that
the truth value of $\varphi$ at every world equals its truth value at
$\at$. Conversely, if $\varphi$ has the same truth value at every
world, then in particular its value at each $v$ agrees with its value
at $\at$.
\end{proof}

In universal models, therefore, the necessitation of the actuality
biconditional fails exactly when $\varphi$ is contingent. A single
contingent atom suffices to separate the two notions of validity.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:separation}
Suppose that $\Two \in \Kl$, and let $a \in \Phi$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\nGv{\Kl} a \leftrightarrow \Act a$;
\item $\nRWv{\Kl} \Box(a \leftrightarrow \Act a)$;
\item real-world validity in $\Kl$ is not closed under necessitation;
\item real-world validity in $\Kl$ properly extends general validity
  in $\Kl$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, \textup{(i)--(iv)} hold for $\Kl_{\mathrm{all}}$,
$\Kl_{\mathrm{S5}}$ and $\Kl_{\mathrm{u}}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In $\Two$ we have $\Two, 0 \not\models a$, while
$\Two, 0 \models \Act a$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid}, since
$\Two, 1 \models a$. Hence $a \leftrightarrow \Act a$ fails at $0$,
which gives (i). Since $1 R 0$, the formula
$\Box(a \leftrightarrow \Act a)$ fails at the actual world $1$, which
gives (ii). By Theorem~\ref{thm:rw}, $a \leftrightarrow \Act a$ is
real-world valid in $\Kl$; hence (ii) yields (iii), and (i) together
with Proposition~\ref{prop:closure}(i) yields (iv).
\end{proof}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:closure}(iii), the failure of necessitation is
specific to real-world validity. If logical truth is identified with
real-world validity, then $a \leftrightarrow \Act a$ is a logical truth
that is not necessary. If it is identified with general validity, then
$a \leftrightarrow \Act a$ is not a logical truth.

The actuality operator interacts differently with the two notions.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:inert}
For every formula $\varphi$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\RWv{\Kl} \Act\varphi$ if and only if $\RWv{\Kl} \varphi$;
\item $\Gv{\Kl} \Act\varphi$ if and only if $\RWv{\Kl} \varphi$;
\item $\Gv{\Kl_{\mathrm{all}}} \Act\varphi \to \Box\Act\varphi$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid}, the truth value of $\Act\varphi$ at any world
of $\Mod$ is the truth value of $\varphi$ at $\at$; this gives (i) and
(ii). For (iii), if $\Act\varphi$ is true at $w$, then $\varphi$ is
true at $\at$, so $\Act\varphi$ is true at every world, and in
particular at every $v$ with $wRv$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:actually-necessary}
For every formula $\varphi$,
$\Gv{\Kl} \Act(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)$ and
$\Gv{\Kl} \Box\Act(\varphi \leftrightarrow \Act\varphi)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first claim follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:rw} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:inert}(ii), and the second from the first by
Proposition~\ref{prop:closure}(iii).
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:actually-necessary} identifies the necessary truth
associated with the actuality biconditional. It is not necessary that
$\varphi$ holds if and only if $\varphi$ actually holds; it is
necessary, and generally valid, that this biconditional actually holds.

\section{Quantified modal logic}
\label{sec:quantified}

The results of Section~\ref{sec:results} extend to first-order modal
logic with variable domains. The extension brings contingent logical
truths of a new kind, which depend on the domains of a model rather
than on contingent atomic facts.

\begin{definition}
Let $\Pi$ be a set of predicate symbols and let $x, y, z, \ldots$
range over a countable set of variables. The language $\Lang^\forall(\Pi)$
is given by the grammar
\[
  \varphi \Coloneqq P(y_1, \ldots, y_n) \mid y = z \mid \bot \mid
  \neg\varphi \mid \varphi \wedge \varphi \mid \varphi \to \varphi \mid
  \forall y\,\varphi \mid \Box\varphi \mid \Act\varphi
  \qquad (P \in \Pi).
\]
We write $E!y$ for $\neg\forall z\,\neg(z = y)$, where $z$ is a
variable distinct from $y$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
A \emph{quantified model} is a tuple
$\Mod = \langle W, R, \at, D, d, I\rangle$, where $W$, $R$ and $\at$
are as in Section~\ref{sec:logic}, $D$ is a set of objects,
$d\colon W \to \mathcal{P}(D)$ assigns to each world its domain, and
$I$ assigns to each predicate symbol $P$ and world $w$ a set
$I(P,w)$ of finite sequences of objects. An \emph{assignment} is a map
$g$ from variables to $D$, and $g[y \mapsto a]$ is the assignment that
differs from $g$ at most in sending $y$ to $a$. Truth at a world under
an assignment is defined by
\begin{align*}
  \Mod, w, g &\models P(y_1, \ldots, y_n) &&\text{iff}\quad
    (g(y_1), \ldots, g(y_n)) \in I(P,w),\\
  \Mod, w, g &\models y = z &&\text{iff}\quad g(y) = g(z),\\
  \Mod, w, g &\models \forall y\,\varphi &&\text{iff}\quad
    \Mod, w, g[y \mapsto a] \models \varphi \text{ for all } a \in d(w),\\
  \Mod, w, g &\models \Box\varphi &&\text{iff}\quad
    \Mod, v, g \models \varphi \text{ for all } v \text{ with } wRv,\\
  \Mod, w, g &\models \Act\varphi &&\text{iff}\quad
    \Mod, \at, g \models \varphi,
\end{align*}
with the Boolean clauses as before.
\end{definition}

The quantifier ranges over the objects that exist at the world of
evaluation, while a free variable may denote an object that does not
exist there. Accordingly, $\Mod, w, g \models E!y$ if and only if
$g(y) \in d(w)$. A formula is \emph{real-world valid} in a class
$\Kl$ of quantified models if it is true at the actual world of every
model in $\Kl$ under every assignment, and \emph{generally valid} in
$\Kl$ if it is true at every world of every model in $\Kl$ under every
assignment. We write $\Kl^\forall_{\mathrm{all}}$ for the class of all
quantified models, $\Kl^\forall_{\mathrm{u}}$ for those with universal
accessibility, and $\Kl^\forall_{\mathrm{c}}$ for those with constant
domains, in which $d(w) = d(v)$ for all worlds $w, v$.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:q-closure}
Let $\Kl$ be a class of quantified models. Proposition~\ref{prop:closure},
Theorems~\ref{thm:rw} and~\ref{thm:characterization}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:inert}\textup{(i)--(ii)} hold for
$\Lang^\forall(\Pi)$ and $\Kl$, with truth at a world taken under an
arbitrary assignment $g$. Moreover, if $\RWv{\Kl} \varphi$, then
$\RWv{\Kl} \forall y\,\varphi$, and likewise for general validity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The proofs of Section~\ref{sec:results} go through with $g$ fixed,
since $\Box$ and $\Act$ do not change the assignment. For the last
claim, validity quantifies over all assignments, so $\varphi$ is true
under $g[y \mapsto a]$ for every $a$.
\end{proof}

We consider three formulas: the commutation
$\Act\forall y\,\varphi \leftrightarrow \forall y\,\Act\varphi$ of the
actuality operator with the quantifier; $\forall y\,\Act E!y$,
``everything actually exists''; and $\forall y\, E!y$, ``everything
exists''.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:q-truths}
Let $\Mod$ be a quantified model, $w$ a world of $\Mod$ and $g$ an
assignment.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\Mod, w, g \models \Act\forall y\,\varphi \leftrightarrow
  \forall y\,\Act\varphi$ if and only if $\varphi$ holds at $\at$ under
  $g[y \mapsto a]$ for all $a \in d(\at)$ exactly when it does so for
  all $a \in d(w)$. Hence the commutation is real-world valid in every
  class, and generally valid in $\Kl^\forall_{\mathrm{c}}$.
\item $\Mod, w, g \models \forall y\,\Act E!y$ if and only if
  $d(w) \subseteq d(\at)$, and
  $\Mod, w, g \models \Box\forall y\,\Act E!y$ if and only if
  $d(v) \subseteq d(\at)$ for every $v$ with $wRv$. Hence
  $\forall y\,\Act E!y$ is real-world valid in every class, and
  generally valid in $\Kl^\forall_{\mathrm{c}}$.
\item $\forall y\, E!y$ is generally valid in
  $\Kl^\forall_{\mathrm{all}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) By the truth clauses, $\Act\forall y\,\varphi$ holds at $w$ if and
only if $\varphi$ holds at $\at$ under $g[y \mapsto a]$ for all
$a \in d(\at)$, and $\forall y\,\Act\varphi$ holds at $w$ if and only
if the same is true for all $a \in d(w)$. At $w = \at$, and in models
with constant domains, the two coincide.
(ii) $\Act E!y$ holds under $g[y \mapsto a]$ if and only if
$a \in d(\at)$, which gives the first claim, and the second follows by
the clause for $\Box$. The inclusion is trivial at $\at$ and holds
everywhere when domains are constant.
(iii) Under $g[y \mapsto a]$ with $a \in d(w)$, the formula $E!y$
holds at $w$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
Let $\mathfrak{G} = \langle \{0,1\}, \{0,1\}^2, 1, \{0,1\}, d_G,
I_G\rangle$, where $d_G(1) = \{1\}$, $d_G(0) = \{0,1\}$, and
$I_G(P,w)$ contains every sequence. Thus $\mathfrak{G}$ has two
mutually accessible worlds, every predicate has the same extension at
both, and the non-actual world has an object that does not actually
exist; see Figure~\ref{fig:growing}. Clearly
$\mathfrak{G} \in \Kl^\forall_{\mathrm{u}}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:q-separation}
Suppose that $\mathfrak{G} \in \Kl$. Then neither
$\forall y\,\Act E!y$ nor $\Act\forall y\,E!y \leftrightarrow
\forall y\,\Act E!y$ is generally valid in $\Kl$, and the
necessitation of neither is real-world valid in $\Kl$. In particular,
real-world validity in $\Kl^\forall_{\mathrm{u}}$ is not closed under
necessitation, although both formulas are generally valid in
$\Kl^\forall_{\mathrm{c}}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In $\mathfrak{G}$, $d_G(0) \not\subseteq d_G(1)$, so by
Theorem~\ref{thm:q-truths}(ii) the formula $\forall y\,\Act E!y$ fails
at $0$; since $1 R 0$, its necessitation fails at $1$. For the
commutation with $\varphi = E!y$, the formula $E!y$ holds at $1$ under
$g[y \mapsto a]$ for every $a \in d_G(1)$, but not for $a = 0 \in
d_G(0)$, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:q-truths}(i) the commutation fails at
$0$, and its necessitation fails at $1$.
\end{proof}

The contrast between Theorem~\ref{thm:q-truths}(iii) and
Theorem~\ref{thm:q-separation} is instructive. ``Everything exists'' is
generally valid and hence necessary, because the quantifier ranges over
what exists at each world. ``Everything actually exists'' is a logical
truth only in the real-world sense: it fails at any world with an
object that does not actually exist. In $\mathfrak{G}$ every predicate
is rigid, so the failure of necessitation owes nothing to contingent
atomic facts; it is produced by the variation of the domains alone.

\section{Actuality in a theory of situations and worlds}
\label{sec:theory}

We now consider a theory $\T$ of situations and worlds in the tradition
of Zalta's theory of abstract objects \cite{zalta1983,zalta1988book}
and his development of situation and world theory \cite{zalta1993}. The
theory is formulated in the metalanguage of higher-order logic.

\subsection{Signature}

The theory has three sorts: a domain $\Dom$ of \emph{objects}, which
also serve as indices of modal evaluation; a sort $\Prop$ of
\emph{propositions}; and a sort $\Pty$ of \emph{properties}. Its
primitive vocabulary consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item operations $\pneg\colon \Prop \to \Prop$ and
  $\pand, \por, \pimp, \piff\colon \Prop \times \Prop \to \Prop$;
\item a map $\VAC\colon \Prop \to \Pty$, sending $p$ to the property
  of being such that $p$;
\item a relation $\Conc \subseteq \Dom \times \Dom$, where
  $\Conc(w,x)$ expresses that $x$ is concrete at the index $w$;
\item encoding relations $\Enc \subseteq \Dom \times \Pty$ and
  $\Encp \subseteq \Dom \times \Prop$;
\item \emph{truth in a situation}, $\models\ \subseteq \Dom \times
  \Prop$, written $s \models p$;
\item \emph{obtaining at an index}, $\Obt \subseteq \Dom \times
  \Prop$, where $\Obt(v,p)$ expresses that $p$ is the case at $v$;
\item a constant $\at \in \Dom$, the actual index.
\end{enumerate}

The modal operators act on conditions on indices. Accessibility is
universal: for $P \subseteq \Dom$, $(\Box P)(w)$ holds if and only if
$P(v)$ for every $v \in \Dom$, and $\Diamond P = \neg\Box\neg P$. The
operators $\Box$ and $\Diamond$ thus satisfy S5 without further axioms.
We use the following abbreviations:
\begin{align*}
  s \partof s' &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\; \forall F\,
    (\Enc(s,F) \to \Enc(s',F)) &&\text{(parthood)}\\
  \Ord(x) &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\;
    \Diamond(\lambda w.\,\Conc(w,x))(x) &&\text{(ordinary object)}\\
  \Abs(x) &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\; \neg\Ord(x)
    &&\text{(abstract object)}\\
  \Obj(x) &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\; \Ord(x) \vee \Abs(x)
    &&\text{(object)}\\
  \Sit(x) &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\; \Abs(x) \wedge \forall F\,
    (\Enc(x,F) \to \exists p\, F = \VAC(p)) &&\text{(situation)}\\
  \Max(s) &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\; \forall p\,
    (s \models p \;\vee\; s \models \pneg p) &&\text{(maximality)}\\
  \Cons(s) &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\; \neg\exists p\,
    (s \models p \;\wedge\; s \models \pneg p) &&\text{(consistency)}
\end{align*}

\subsection{Axioms}

The axioms of $\T$ fall into four groups. Free variables are
universally quantified.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(T\arabic*), leftmargin=3.2em]
\item \label{ax:boolean} \emph{Propositional identities.} The
  operations on $\Prop$ satisfy double negation, the De Morgan laws,
  commutativity and associativity of $\pand$ and $\por$, and the
  identities $p \pimp q = \pneg p \por q$ and
  $p \piff q = (p \pimp q) \pand (q \pimp p)$.
\item \label{ax:encoding} \emph{Encoding and truth.} If $\Obj(x)$, then
  $\Encp(x,p) \leftrightarrow \Enc(x, \VAC(p))$ and
  $x \models p \leftrightarrow \Encp(x,p)$.
\item \label{ax:obtains} \emph{Obtaining.} For every index $v$,
  \begin{align*}
    \Obt(v, \pneg p) &\leftrightarrow \neg\Obt(v,p), &
    \Obt(v, p \pand q) &\leftrightarrow \Obt(v,p) \wedge \Obt(v,q),\\
    \Obt(v, p \por q) &\leftrightarrow \Obt(v,p) \vee \Obt(v,q), &
    \Obt(v, p \pimp q) &\leftrightarrow (\Obt(v,p) \to \Obt(v,q)).
  \end{align*}
  If $\VAC(p) = \VAC(q)$, then $\Obt(v,p) \leftrightarrow \Obt(v,q)$.
\item \label{ax:abstract} \emph{Abstract objects.} Ordinary objects
  necessarily encode no property:
  $\Ord(x) \to \Box(\lambda w.\,\neg\exists F\,\Enc(x,F))(x)$. For
  every condition $\varphi$ on properties there is an abstract object
  encoding exactly the properties that satisfy it:
  $\exists x\,(\Abs(x) \wedge \forall F\,(\Enc(x,F) \leftrightarrow
  \varphi(F)))$. Abstract objects that encode the same properties are
  identical.
\end{enumerate}

Axiom group~\ref{ax:obtains} imposes the classical truth conditions on
obtaining and not on truth in a situation, so that the latter may be
partial; Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} shows that this is
unavoidable. The last clause of~\ref{ax:obtains} states that equal
vacuous properties have the same obtaining conditions. In Zalta's theory
it follows from $\beta$-conversion: an object exemplifies the property
of being such that $p$ if and only if $p$. Since $\T$ has no
exemplification relation, the consequence is postulated directly. It is
weaker than injectivity of $\VAC$.

\subsection{Situations and the actual world}

By the comprehension and identity principles in~\ref{ax:abstract},
there is a unique abstract object $\aw$ such that
\[
  \Enc(\aw, F) \;\leftrightarrow\; \exists p\,
    \bigl(F = \VAC(p) \wedge \Obt(\at, p)\bigr)
\]
for every property $F$. We call $\aw$ the \emph{actual world}. In the
same way, for objects $s$ and $s'$ let the \emph{meet} $s \meet s'$ and
the \emph{join} $s \join s'$ be the unique abstract objects such that
\[
  \Enc(s \meet s', F) \leftrightarrow \Enc(s,F) \wedge \Enc(s',F),
  \qquad
  \Enc(s \join s', F) \leftrightarrow \Enc(s,F) \vee \Enc(s',F).
\]
The basic principles of situations, of the actual world and of meet
and join are then theorems of $\T$, rather than axioms.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:situations}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Every situation is an object, and every property encoded by a
  situation is of the form $\VAC(p)$.
\item Situations that make the same propositions true are identical.
\item If $\Sit(s)$, $\Abs(x)$ and $x \partof s$, then $\Sit(x)$.
\item $\Sit(\aw)$, and $\aw \models p \leftrightarrow \Obt(\at, p)$ for
  every $p$.
\item If $\Sit(s)$ and $\Sit(s')$, then $\Sit(s \meet s')$ and
  $\Sit(s \join s')$. For all $s, s', u$:
  $(s \meet s') \models p \leftrightarrow (s \models p \wedge
  s' \models p)$; $s \partof s \join s'$ and $s' \partof s \join s'$;
  and if $s \partof u$ and $s' \partof u$, then $s \join s' \partof u$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every object $x$, \ref{ax:encoding} gives
$x \models p \leftrightarrow \Enc(x, \VAC(p))$, and every $x$ is an
object, since $\Abs = \neg\Ord$.
(i) is immediate from the definition of $\Sit$.
(ii) Let $s, s'$ be situations that make the same propositions true. If
$\Enc(s,F)$, then $F = \VAC(p)$ for some $p$ by (i), so $s \models p$,
hence $s' \models p$ and $\Enc(s',F)$; and symmetrically. Thus $s$ and
$s'$ are abstract objects encoding the same properties, and they are
identical by~\ref{ax:abstract}.
(iii) Every property encoded by $x$ is encoded by $s$, and is therefore
of the form $\VAC(p)$.
(iv) $\aw$ is abstract and encodes only properties of the form
$\VAC(p)$. Moreover, $\aw \models p$ if and only if
$\Obt(\at, q)$ for some $q$ with $\VAC(p) = \VAC(q)$, which by the last
clause of~\ref{ax:obtains} is equivalent to $\Obt(\at, p)$.
(v) The meet and join of situations are abstract and encode only
properties encoded by $s$ or $s'$, hence only properties of the form
$\VAC(p)$. The truth condition of the meet follows from
$x \models p \leftrightarrow \Enc(x, \VAC(p))$, and the parthood claims
from the encoding conditions of the join.
\end{proof}

Part~(iv) relates the actual world to the actual index: what is true in
$\aw$ is exactly what is the case.

\subsection{Worlds}

Following Zalta, a world is a situation that could make true exactly
what is the case, and a situation is actual if everything true in it is
the case. Since the propositions of $\T$ do not themselves carry truth
values, ``$p$ is the case'' is expressed by obtaining at the index of
evaluation:
\begin{align*}
  \World(s) &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\; \Sit(s) \wedge
    \Diamond\bigl(\lambda v.\,\forall p\,
      (s \models p \leftrightarrow \Obt(v,p))\bigr)(\at),\\
  \Actual(s) &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\; \forall p\,
    (s \models p \to \Obt(\at, p)),\\
  \Poss(s) &\;:\Longleftrightarrow\;
    \Diamond\bigl(\lambda v.\,\forall p\,
      (s \models p \to \Obt(v,p))\bigr)(\at).
\end{align*}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:worlds}
The following are theorems of $\T$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If $\World(w)$, there is an index $v$ such that
  $w \models p \leftrightarrow \Obt(v,p)$ for every $p$.
\item If $\World(w)$, then $w \models \pneg p \leftrightarrow
  \neg(w \models p)$, and truth at $w$ satisfies the classical clauses
  for $\pand$, $\por$ and $\pimp$.
\item If $\World(w)$, then $\Max(w)$, $\Cons(w)$ and $\Poss(w)$.
\item If $\Poss(s)$, then $\Cons(s)$.
\item $\World(s) \leftrightarrow \Sit(s) \wedge \Max(s) \wedge \Poss(s)$.
\item $\World(\aw)$ and $\Actual(\aw)$. If $\Actual(s)$, then
  $\Poss(s)$ and $\Cons(s)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) Since accessibility is universal, $(\Diamond P)(\at)$ is equivalent
to $\exists v\, P(v)$.
(ii) Let $v$ be as in (i); the clauses transfer from~\ref{ax:obtains}.
(iii) Maximality and consistency follow from the clause for $\pneg$ in
(ii), and the index $v$ of (i) witnesses $\Poss(w)$.
(iv) Let $v$ witness $\Poss(s)$. If $s \models p$ and
$s \models \pneg p$, then $\Obt(v,p)$ and $\Obt(v,\pneg p)$, contrary
to~\ref{ax:obtains}.
(v) The forward direction is (iii). Conversely, let $v$ witness
$\Poss(s)$; it suffices to show that $\Obt(v,p)$ implies
$s \models p$. By maximality, $s \models p$ or $s \models \pneg p$, and
the second alternative yields $\Obt(v,\pneg p)$, which is incompatible
with $\Obt(v,p)$.
(vi) By Proposition~\ref{prop:situations}(iv), the index $\at$
witnesses $\World(\aw)$, and $\Actual(\aw)$ is one direction of it. If
$\Actual(s)$,
then $\at$ witnesses $\Poss(s)$, and $\Cons(s)$ follows by (iv).
\end{proof}

By part~(v), the worlds are exactly the maximal possible situations.
Consistency need not be required, since by part~(iv) it follows from
possibility.

\subsection{Truth in a situation and truth at an index}

The distinction between truth in a situation and obtaining at an index
is essential to $\T$. If truth in a situation is required to satisfy
the classical clause for negation, the theory collapses.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:collapse}
Let $\T^-$ be the extension of $\T$ by the schema
\[
  s \models \pneg p \;\leftrightarrow\; \neg(s \models p)
  \qquad (s \in \Dom,\ p \in \Prop).
\]
In $\T^-$, any two objects make the same propositions true, and there
is at most one situation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $s, s' \in \Dom$ and $p \in \Prop$. By the schema and the truth
condition of the meet in Proposition~\ref{prop:situations}(v), which
holds for all objects, the truth of $\pneg p$ in $s \meet s'$ can
be computed in two ways:
\[
  \neg\bigl(s \models p \wedge s' \models p\bigr)
  \;\leftrightarrow\; (s \meet s') \models \pneg p
  \;\leftrightarrow\; \neg(s \models p) \wedge \neg(s' \models p).
\]
If exactly one of $s \models p$ and $s' \models p$ holds, the left-hand
side is true and the right-hand side is false. Hence
$s \models p \leftrightarrow s' \models p$. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:situations}(ii), any two situations are
identical.
\end{proof}

The schema of Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} also forces every
situation to be maximal, so that no situation could be partial. A
related difficulty arises if ``$p$ is the case'' in the definition of
worldhood is replaced by a tautology.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:empty}
Let $\World^\top(s)$ abbreviate $\Sit(s) \wedge
\Diamond(\lambda v.\,\forall p\,(s \models p \leftrightarrow
\top))(\at)$. If $\World^\top(\aw)$ and
$\aw \models \pneg p \leftrightarrow \neg(\aw \models p)$ for every
$p$, then $\Prop$ is empty.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The assumption $\World^\top(\aw)$ gives $\aw \models p$ for every $p$,
and in particular $\aw \models p$ and $\aw \models \pneg p$, which
together with the clause for negation is a contradiction for any
$p \in \Prop$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The actuality operator}

In $\T$ the actuality operator is definable. For a condition
$P \subseteq \Dom$ on indices, let
\[
  (\Act P)(w) \;:\Longleftrightarrow\; P(\at).
\]
The operator $\Act$ is rigid and idempotent, and
$\Box\Act P \leftrightarrow \Act P \leftrightarrow \Diamond\Act P$
holds at every index. The actuality of a situation is the
actualization of its possibility condition: for every index $w$,
$\Actual(s)$ holds if and only if
$\Act\bigl(\lambda v.\,\forall p\,(s \models p \to \Obt(v,p))\bigr)(w)$.

A condition $P$ is \emph{necessary} at $w$ if $(\Box P)(w)$,
\emph{impossible} at $w$ if $(\Box\neg P)(w)$, and \emph{contingent} at
$w$ if $(\Diamond P)(w)$ and $(\Diamond\neg P)(w)$.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:theory-actuality}
In $\T$, for every condition $P$ and index $w$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $P(\at) \leftrightarrow (\Act P)(\at)$;
\item $\Box(\lambda v.\, P(v) \leftrightarrow (\Act P)(v))(w)$ holds if
  and only if $P$ is necessary or impossible at $w$;
\item if $P$ is contingent at $w$, then
  $\Box(\lambda v.\, P(v) \leftrightarrow (\Act P)(v))(w)$ fails.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, for every proposition $q$ whose obtaining is contingent
at $\at$, the condition $\Obt(\at,q) \leftrightarrow
\Act(\lambda v.\,\Obt(v,q))(\at)$ holds but is not necessary.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Part (i) is the definition of $\Act$. For (ii), the left-hand side
states that $P(v) \leftrightarrow P(\at)$ for every $v$. If $P(\at)$,
then $P$ is necessary at $w$; otherwise $P$ is impossible at $w$.
Conversely, a necessary or impossible condition is constant on $\Dom$
and therefore agrees everywhere with its value at $\at$. Part (iii)
follows from (ii), since a contingent condition is neither necessary
nor impossible.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The theory as a model of the logic of actuality}

The theory determines a model of the logic of
Section~\ref{sec:logic}, with the propositions of $\T$ as atoms.

\begin{definition}
Let $\Mod_\T = \langle \Dom, \Dom \times \Dom, \at, V_\T\rangle$, with
set of atoms $\Prop$ and $V_\T(q) = \{v \in \Dom : \Obt(v,q)\}$. The
translation $\tr{\cdot}$ from $\Lang(\Prop)$ to conditions on $\Dom$ is
defined by $\tr{q} = \lambda v.\,\Obt(v,q)$, by commutation with the
Boolean connectives, and by $\tr{\Box\varphi} = \Box\tr{\varphi}$ and
$\tr{\Act\varphi} = \Act\tr{\varphi}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:agreement}
For every $\varphi \in \Lang(\Prop)$ and $v \in \Dom$,
$\Mod_\T, v \models \varphi$ if and only if $\tr{\varphi}(v)$.
Consequently, if $\RWv{\Kl_{\mathrm{u}}} \varphi$, then
$\tr{\varphi}(\at)$ is a theorem of $\T$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By induction on $\varphi$. The atomic case is the definition of
$V_\T$, and the Boolean cases are immediate. In the case of
$\Box\varphi$, both sides quantify over all of $\Dom$, and in the case
of $\Act\varphi$, both sides reduce to the induction hypothesis at
$\at$. For the second claim, $\Mod_\T \in \Kl_{\mathrm{u}}$, so
$\Mod_\T, \at \models \varphi$.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:agreement} shows that the operator defined in $\T$ is
the actuality operator of Crossley and Humberstone, and that every
formula real-world valid over universal models holds at the actual
index of $\T$. The theory does not determine whether any proposition is
contingent; this is settled in the model constructed in the next
section.

\subsection{Actualist quantification}
\label{sec:theory-quantified}

The quantifiers of $\T$, like those of Zalta's theory, range over the
single domain $\Dom$, which is the same at every index. Concreteness,
however, is relative to an index, and the objects concrete at an index
$v$, the set $\{x \in \Dom : \Conc(v,x)\}$, may vary with $v$.
Quantification restricted to them is actualist quantification. For a
binary condition $\varphi$ on objects and indices, let
\[
  (\forall^{\Conc} \varphi)(v) \;:\Longleftrightarrow\;
    \forall x\,(\Conc(v,x) \to \varphi(x,v)).
\]
Write $\mathrm{AC}$ for $\forall^{\Conc}(\lambda x.\,\Act(\lambda
v.\,\Conc(v,x)))$, ``everything concrete is actually concrete'', and
$\mathrm{CC}$ for the commutation
$\lambda w.\,\bigl(\Act(\forall^{\Conc}\Conc')(w) \leftrightarrow
\forall^{\Conc}(\lambda x.\,\Act(\Conc'(x)))(w)\bigr)$, where
$\Conc'(x,v)$ is $\Conc(v,x)$.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:theory-quantified}
In $\T$, for every index $w$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\mathrm{AC}(\at)$ and $\mathrm{CC}(\at)$ hold, and
  $\Box\forall^{\Conc}\Conc'$ holds at $w$;
\item $(\Box\,\mathrm{AC})(w)$ holds if and only if
  $\Conc(v,x) \to \Conc(\at,x)$ for all $v$ and $x$;
\item if $\Conc(v,x)$ and $\neg\Conc(\at,x)$ for some $v$ and $x$,
  then neither $(\Box\,\mathrm{AC})(w)$ nor $(\Box\,\mathrm{CC})(w)$
  holds.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) At $\at$, both $\mathrm{AC}$ and $\mathrm{CC}$ compare the
objects concrete at $\at$ with themselves, and
$\forall^{\Conc}\Conc'$ holds at every index by definition.
(ii) The left-hand side states that whatever is concrete at an index
$v$ is concrete at $\at$. (iii) The first claim follows from (ii). For
the second, $\Act(\forall^{\Conc}\Conc')$ holds at $v$ by (i), but
$\forall^{\Conc}(\lambda y.\,\Act(\Conc'(y)))$ fails at $v$, since
$x$ is concrete at $v$ and not at $\at$.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:theory-quantified} is the counterpart in $\T$ of
Theorems~\ref{thm:q-truths} and~\ref{thm:q-separation}. The next
theorem shows that it is an instance of them: $\mathrm{AC}$ is the
translation of $\forall y\,\Act E!y$ into $\T$.

\begin{definition}
Let $\Pi$ be a set of predicate symbols and let $I$ assign to each
$P \in \Pi$ and index $v$ a set $I(P,v)$ of finite sequences of
objects. The quantified model $\Mod^\forall_{\T,I} = \langle \Dom,
\Dom \times \Dom, \at, \Dom, d_\T, I\rangle$ has domains
$d_\T(v) = \{x : \Conc(v,x)\}$. The translation $\tr{\cdot}_I$
sends a formula of $\Lang^\forall(\Pi)$ and an assignment $g$ to a
condition on indices: $\tr{P(y_1,\ldots,y_n)}_I^g(v)$ is
$(g(y_1),\ldots,g(y_n)) \in I(P,v)$, $\tr{y = z}_I^g$ is
$\lambda v.\,g(y) = g(z)$, $\tr{\forall y\,\varphi}_I^g$ is
$\forall^{\Conc}(\lambda a\,v.\,\tr{\varphi}_I^{g[y\mapsto a]}(v))$,
and the translation commutes with the Boolean connectives, $\Box$ and
$\Act$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:q-agreement}
For every $\varphi \in \Lang^\forall(\Pi)$, index $v$ and
assignment $g$, $\Mod^\forall_{\T,I}, v, g \models \varphi$ if and
only if $\tr{\varphi}_I^g(v)$. Consequently, if
$\RWv{\Kl^\forall_{\mathrm{u}}} \varphi$, then
$\tr{\varphi}_I^g(\at)$ is a theorem of $\T$ for every $I$ and $g$.
Moreover, $\tr{\forall y\,\Act E!y}_I^g(v)$ holds if and only if
$\mathrm{AC}(v)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By induction on $\varphi$, generalizing over $v$ and $g$, as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:agreement}; in the case of $\forall y\,\varphi$
both sides range over the objects concrete at $v$. The last claim
holds by the first and Theorem~\ref{thm:q-truths}(ii), since $E!y$ holds at $v$
under $g$ exactly when $\Conc(v, g(y))$.
\end{proof}

The possibilist quantifier of $\T$ commutes with $\Box$: for every
binary condition $\varphi$ and index $w$,
$\forall x\,(\Box\varphi(x))(w) \leftrightarrow
\Box(\lambda v.\,\forall x\,\varphi(x,v))(w)$, so both the Barcan
formula \cite{barcan1946} and its converse hold for it. For the
actualist quantifier they are conditions on the domains. Let
$\mathrm{BF}_\varphi(w)$ be
$\forall^{\Conc}(\lambda x.\,\Box\varphi(x))(w) \to
(\Box\forall^{\Conc}\varphi)(w)$ and let $\mathrm{CBF}_\varphi(w)$ be
its converse.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:barcan}
In $\T$, for every index $w$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\mathrm{BF}_\varphi(w)$ holds for every $\varphi$ if and only if
  $\Conc(v,x) \to \Conc(w,x)$ for all $v$ and $x$;
\item $\mathrm{CBF}_\varphi(w)$ holds for every $\varphi$ if and only if
  $\Conc(w,x) \to \Conc(v,x)$ for all $v$ and $x$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Suppose that every possible domain is included in the domain at
$w$, and that $\Box\varphi(x)$ holds at $w$ for every $x$ concrete at
$w$. If $x$ is concrete at an index $v$, then $x$ is concrete at $w$,
so $\varphi(x,v)$; this gives $\Box\forall^{\Conc}\varphi$ at $w$. Conversely, take
$\varphi(x,v) = \Conc(w,x)$: the antecedent holds trivially, and the
consequent is the inclusion. (ii) If the domain at $w$ is included in
every domain, then $\Box\forall^{\Conc}\varphi$ yields $\varphi(x)$ at
every index for every $x$ concrete at $w$. Conversely, take
$\varphi = \Conc'$: the antecedent holds by
Theorem~\ref{thm:theory-quantified}(i), and the consequent is the
inclusion.
\end{proof}

The theory does not decide whether the domain of concrete objects
varies. The model of the next section shows that it may.

\section{A model of the theory}
\label{sec:model}

We construct a model $\N$ of $\T$. Let $\mathbb{B} = \{0,1\}$ be a set
of two valuations, with $1$ the actual valuation. A proposition is a
pair $p = (|p|, t)$ of a set $|p| \subseteq \mathbb{B}$ of valuations
and a \emph{tag} $t \in \{0,1\}$. For $b \in \mathbb{B}$ let
$S_b = \{p : b \in |p|\}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Prop = \Pty = \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B}) \times \{0,1\}$, and
  $\VAC$ is the identity. The operations act on the sets of valuations
  as the Boolean operations and combine the tags by maximum:
  $\pneg(P,t) = (\mathbb{B} \setminus P, t)$,
  $(P,t) \pand (Q,u) = (P \cap Q, \max(t,u))$,
  $(P,t) \por (Q,u) = (P \cup Q, \max(t,u))$,
  $p \pimp q = \pneg p \por q$ and
  $p \piff q = (p \pimp q) \pand (q \pimp p)$.
\item $\Dom = \{\iota_b : b \in \mathbb{B}\} \sqcup
  \{\alpha_S : S \subseteq \Pty\}$. The elements $\iota_b$ are the
  \emph{indices} and the elements $\alpha_S$ are the \emph{abstract
  objects}.
\item $\Conc(w,\iota_b)$ holds if and only if $w = \iota_0$ or
  $b = 1$, and $\Conc(w,\alpha_S)$ never holds. Thus only $\iota_1$
  is concrete at $\at$, and both indices are concrete at $\iota_0$.
  $\Enc(\iota_b, F)$ never holds, $\Enc(\alpha_S, F)$ holds if and only
  if $F \in S$, and $\Encp(x,p)$ holds if and only if $\Enc(x,p)$.
\item $x \models p$ if and only if $\Encp(x,p)$; thus
  $\alpha_S \models p$ if and only if $p \in S$.
\item $\Obt(\iota_b, p)$ if and only if $b \in |p|$, and
  $\Obt(\alpha_S, p)$ if and only if $1 \in |p|$.
\item $\at = \iota_1$.
\end{itemize}
Obtaining ignores the tag. Since every property is of the form
$\VAC(p)$, the situations of $\N$ are exactly the abstract objects
$\alpha_S$. The actual world is $\aw = \alpha_{S_1}$, since
$\Obt(\iota_1, p)$ if and only if $1 \in |p|$, and the meet and join of
$x$ and $y$ are $\alpha_{\{F : \Enc(x,F) \wedge \Enc(y,F)\}}$ and
$\alpha_{\{F : \Enc(x,F) \vee \Enc(y,F)\}}$.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:model}
$\N$ satisfies every axiom of $\T$. Consequently, $\T$ is consistent
relative to higher-order logic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We verify the axiom groups in turn.
\ref{ax:boolean} holds because $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B})$ is a Boolean
algebra, negation preserves tags, and maximum is commutative and
associative.
\ref{ax:encoding} holds by definition.
\ref{ax:obtains} holds because, for fixed $v$, $\Obt(v,\cdot)$ is
membership of a fixed valuation, and because $\VAC$ is the identity.
For~\ref{ax:abstract}: every index is concrete at $\iota_0$, hence
ordinary, and encodes no property; no $\alpha_S$ is concrete at any
index, so every $\alpha_S$ is abstract. Comprehension for a condition
$\varphi$ is witnessed by $\alpha_{\{F : \varphi(F)\}}$, and abstract
objects encoding the same properties have the same index set $S$ and
are therefore identical.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:nondegenerate}
In $\N$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\aw = \alpha_{S_1}$ and $\alpha_{S_0}$ are distinct worlds;
\item the proposition $c = (\{1\}, 0)$ is contingent: it obtains at
  $\iota_1$ and does not obtain at $\iota_0$;
\item the situation $\alpha_\varnothing$ is partial and possible but is
  not a world, and $\alpha_\varnothing \partof \aw$ with
  $\alpha_\varnothing \neq \aw$;
\item the situation $\alpha_{\Pty}$ is not possible;
\item there are both ordinary and abstract objects;
\item the propositions $c$ and $c' = (\{1\}, 1)$ are distinct but
  necessarily equivalent, and the possible situation $\alpha_{\{c\}}$
  makes $c$ true but not $c'$;
\item the domain of concrete objects grows: $\iota_0$ is concrete at
  $\iota_0$ but not at $\at$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) The index $\iota_b$ witnesses $\World(\alpha_{S_b})$, and
$c \in S_1 \setminus S_0$. (ii) is immediate.
(iii) $\alpha_\varnothing$ makes neither $c$ nor $\pneg c$ true; it is
possible because it makes nothing true; it is not a world by
Theorem~\ref{thm:worlds}(iii), since it is not maximal; and it encodes
no property, so it is a part of every object.
(iv) $\alpha_{\Pty}$ makes both $c$ and $\pneg c$ true, so it is not
consistent, and hence not possible by Theorem~\ref{thm:worlds}(iv).
(v) The indices are ordinary and the $\alpha_S$ are abstract.
(vi) $c$ and $c'$ differ in their tag, and $\Obt(v, c)$ and
$\Obt(v, c')$ both depend only on $\{1\}$. The situation
$\alpha_{\{c\}}$ makes only $c$ true, and $c$ obtains at $\iota_1$.
(vii) is immediate from the definition of $\Conc$.
\end{proof}

By part~(vi), the identities~\ref{ax:boolean} do not make the
propositions of $\T$ intensional: necessarily equivalent propositions
may be distinct, and situations may distinguish them. Truth in a
situation is therefore hyperintensional in $\N$, as in Zalta's theory,
although the propositional identities hold. Figure~\ref{fig:situations}
shows the situations of Theorem~\ref{thm:nondegenerate} and their

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:contingent-truth}
In $\N$, the condition
$\Obt(\at,c) \leftrightarrow \Act(\lambda v.\,\Obt(v,c))(\at)$ holds,
but its necessitation fails: the proposition $c$ does not obtain at the
index $\iota_0$, although it actually obtains.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:theory-actuality} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:nondegenerate}(ii).
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:contingent-truth} exhibits the phenomenon of
Section~\ref{sec:results} in a model of the full theory, in which
situations may be partial, there is more than one world, and ordinary
and abstract objects coexist. By Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}, no
model of $\T^-$ has two distinct worlds.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:q-contingent-truth}
In $\N$, ``everything concrete is actually concrete'' holds at $\at$,
but its necessitation fails, and so does the necessitation of the
commutation $\mathrm{CC}$: the object $\iota_0$ is concrete at
$\iota_0$ but not actually concrete.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:theory-quantified} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:nondegenerate}(vii).
\end{proof}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:barcan}, both Barcan formulas fail in $\N$
for the actualist quantifier: $\mathrm{BF}$ fails at $\at$, for
$\varphi(x,v) = \Conc(\at,x)$, because $\iota_0$ could be concrete
without being actually concrete, and $\mathrm{CBF}$ fails at
$\iota_0$, for $\varphi = \Conc'$, because $\iota_0$ is concrete there
but not at $\at$. Both hold for the possibilist quantifier.

The domains of concrete objects in $\N$ are those of the model
$\mathfrak{G}$ of Section~\ref{sec:quantified}, with $\iota_b$ in place
of $b$. As there, the failure of necessitation in
Corollary~\ref{cor:q-contingent-truth} owes nothing to which
propositions obtain: it is produced by the variation of the domain
alone.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

The actuality operator and its semantics are due to Crossley and
Humberstone \cite{crossley1977}. The distinction between real-world and
general validity, and the failure of necessitation for the former, are
due to Davies and Humberstone \cite{davies1980}, who also introduced the
operator ``fixedly''. Kaplan's logic of demonstratives
\cite{kaplan1989} exhibits a closely related phenomenon: sentences true
in every context of utterance that nonetheless express contingent
propositions. Zalta \cite{zalta1988} argued that real-world validity
explicates logical truth, so that some logical truths are not
necessary; Hanson \cite{hanson2006} defended general validity, and
Nelson and Zalta \cite{nelson2012} replied. The results of
Section~\ref{sec:results} are neutral between these positions. They
make precise what each position is committed to, and they identify the
necessary truth associated with the actuality biconditional
(Corollary~\ref{cor:actually-necessary}).

The theory of Section~\ref{sec:theory} follows Zalta's theory of
abstract objects \cite{zalta1983,zalta1988book} and his treatment of
situations and worlds \cite{zalta1993}. Fitelson and Zalta
\cite{fitelson2007} verified a number of theorems of situation and world
theory with an automated reasoning system. Kirchner, Benzm\"uller and
Zalta \cite{kirchner2019,kirchner2020} embedded the higher-order
fragment of Zalta's \emph{Principia Logico-Metaphysica}, which includes
an actuality operator, in Isabelle/HOL by the method of shallow
semantical embeddings \cite{benzmuller2013}. Their embedding
distinguishes modally strict theorems, to which necessitation applies,
from theorems that depend on the axioms of actuality, and its
construction led to the discovery and resolution of a paradox in the
theory. The present work differs in treating real-world and general
validity as properties of formulas relative to arbitrary classes of
models, in characterizing exactly when the actuality biconditional is
necessary, and in exhibiting the phenomenon in a model of a theory of
situations that separates truth in a situation from truth at an index.

Linsky and Zalta \cite{linsky1994} defended the simplest quantified
modal logic, with a constant domain, the Barcan formula and its
converse, by reading the contingency of existence as the contingency of
concreteness: what could have been concrete is not thereby a merely
possible object, but a necessarily existing object that is contingently
nonconcrete. The treatment of quantification in
Section~\ref{sec:theory-quantified} formalizes this reading. The
quantifiers of $\T$ range over a constant domain and satisfy both
Barcan formulas, while actualist quantification is quantification
restricted to the concrete objects at an index; for it, the Barcan
formulas are conditions on how the domains vary
(Proposition~\ref{prop:barcan}), and both fail in $\N$. The quantified
contingent logical truths of Section~\ref{sec:quantified} then arise,
as Linsky and Zalta's reading predicts, from contingent concreteness
alone (Corollary~\ref{cor:q-contingent-truth}).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For every class of models, general validity is closed under
necessitation and real-world validity is not, and the two notions
separate on every class containing a two-world model. The actuality
biconditional is real-world valid, its necessitation holds exactly
where its argument is constant, and its actualization is generally
valid. These facts carry over to quantified modal logic with variable
domains, where ``everything actually exists'' is real-world valid but
not necessary, even when every predicate is rigid, while ``everything
exists'' is generally valid.

The same holds for the definable actuality operator of a theory of
situations and worlds. That theory is a model of the logic of
actuality, and it has a non-degenerate model in which the biconditional
for a contingent proposition is true but not necessary. The theory
requires truth in a situation to be distinguished from truth at an
index. Situations, the actual world, and meet and join are defined as
in Zalta's theory, and the principles that govern them are theorems;
the only postulate this adds to the theory of encoding and abstract
objects is that equal vacuous properties have the same obtaining
conditions, which in Zalta's theory follows from $\beta$-conversion.
Although the propositional identities hold, the theory does not make
propositions intensional: its model has distinct but necessarily
equivalent propositions, and situations that distinguish them.

The domain of $\T$ is constant, but concreteness is relative to an
index, and actualist quantification over the concrete objects at an
index is definable. With it, $\T$ is a model of quantified modal logic
with variable domains, and the quantified contingent truths of
Section~\ref{sec:quantified} arise within the theory: ``everything
concrete is actually concrete'' holds at the actual index, and in the
model of $\T$ it is not necessary. The possibilist quantifiers satisfy
both Barcan formulas, as in the simplest quantified modal logic of
Linsky and Zalta, while for the actualist quantifier the Barcan
formulas are conditions on the domains, and both fail in the model. No
postulate is needed for this.

One direction remains. With a primitive exemplification relation
satisfying $\beta$-conversion, the remaining postulate on vacuous
properties should become derivable, as it is in Zalta's theory.
