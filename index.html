Possible-World Semantics

A Mechanistic Formalization in Dependent Type Theory

Haniel Ulises Repository Paper (PDF) Lean 4 · v4.26.0

Abstract

This project presents a formalization of possible-world and situation semantics within Lean 4's dependent type theory, following the higher-order modal and situation-theoretic tradition of Zalta, Fine, and Barwise–Perry. The development proceeds entirely from primitive typed constants, without recourse to any external mathematical library.

The distinguishing commitment of the formalization is mechanistic grounding: wherever a logical principle admits derivation from antecedent definitions, it is established as a theorem rather than postulated. This discipline is most consequential in the modal layer, where the entirety of S5 — including the necessitation rule and the characteristic collapse ◊□φ → □φ — is derived from a single definitional choice in which the accessibility relation is the universal relation on the domain of worlds.

The ontological core follows Zalta's abstract object theory. Situations and worlds are not type-distinct; situationhood and worldhood are predicates over a single domain. Encoding is the primitive relation from which parthood, propositional truth, and the mereology of situations are all constructed. The resulting theory proves Theorems 4–6 of Zalta (1993) — the equivalence of parthood and truth-inclusion, antisymmetry of parthood, and same-parts identity — as mechanically verified results. Truth in a situation, which may be partial, is distinguished from obtaining at an index, which is classical; worlds are defined as in Zalta, as situations that could make true exactly what is the case, and are proved to be precisely the maximal possible situations.

The theory admits a definable actuality operator 𝒜. For an arbitrary class of Kripke models with a designated world, real-world validity is shown not to be closed under necessitation: φ ↔ 𝒜φ is real-world valid, whereas □(φ ↔ 𝒜φ) holds exactly where φ is constant. A non-degenerate model of all the axioms, in which this biconditional is true but not necessary for a contingent proposition, establishes the consistency of the theory. In that model necessarily equivalent propositions may be distinct, so the theory does not make propositions intensional. The results on validity extend to quantified modal logic with variable domains, where “everything actually exists” is real-world valid but not necessary.

Theoretical Framework

The ontology is stratified into three typed domains: a domain of worlds (which comprises both possible worlds and proper situations), a domain of properties, and a domain of propositions. The predicate Situation, defined as in Zalta, carves out the sub-domain of situations within the world domain; Worldhood further identifies those situations that could make true exactly what is the case.

The central primitive is the encoding relation, written enc(s, F), between a situation and a property. A situation encodes exactly those properties of the form VAC(p) — the vacuous abstraction of some proposition p. Following Zalta, this is the definition of a situation: an abstract object that encodes only such properties. Parthood is defined encoding-first:

s ⊴ s′  :⟺  ∀F. enc(s, F) → enc(s′, F)

Truth-in, written s ⊨ p, is a separate primitive governed by the reduction: a situation s makes p true if and only if s encodes VAC(p). The bridge between the two orderings — encoding-based parthood and truth-based inclusion — is the content of Theorem 4:

s ⊴ s′  ↔  ∀p. (s ⊨ p → s′ ⊨ p)

Truth-in is partial: a situation may make neither p nor its negation true. The classical truth conditions are borne instead by a second primitive, obtaining, written Ob(v, p) and read “p is the case at the index v”, where the indices are the points over which the modal operators quantify:

Ob(v, ¬p)  ↔  ¬Ob(v, p)   Ob(v, p ∧ q)  ↔  Ob(v, p) ∧ Ob(v, q)

The separation is not optional. Were truth-in itself required to satisfy the classical clause for negation, the truth conditions of the meet would force any two situations to agree on every proposition, and extensionality would leave at most one situation.

Modal operators are grounded in a Kripke frame whose accessibility relation R is the universal relation: Rwv holds for all worlds w, v. This is the characteristic frame condition of S5. Necessity and possibility are then defined semantically:

□φ(w)  :⟺  ∀v. Rwv → φ(v) ◊φ(w)  :⟺  ¬□¬φ(w)

Because R is definitionally universal, □φ(w) reduces to ∀v. φ(v), making the modal operators fully transparent to the kernel. Reflexivity, transitivity, and symmetry of R are consequences of its definition, not independent postulates. All S5 schemas and the necessitation rule are theorems derivable from this single definitional choice.

Ontological dependence is defined in the tradition of Fine and Correia: a situation s depends on s′, written s ≺ s′, when every situation that includes s also includes s′:

s ≺ s′  :⟺  ∀u. Sit(u) → s ⊴ u → s′ ⊴ u

The meet of two situations is their greatest lower bound under ⊴, characterised by the principle that s ∧ s′ makes precisely the propositions true that both s and s′ make true. The three lattice properties — s ∧ s′ ⊴ s, s ∧ s′ ⊴ s′, and the universal property — are established as theorems once the equivalence of parthood and truth-inclusion is available.

Formal Signature

The theory is formulated within Lean 4's dependent type theory over a primitive ontological domain W. No set-theoretic reduction of this domain is presupposed. Situations and worlds are not type-distinct entities but are instead distinguished by predicates over a common domain. Alongside W, the theory contains a domain of properties P and a domain of propositions Π.

The primitive non-logical vocabulary consists of the relations enc, ⊨, Ob and E (concreteness at an index), the operation VAC, and the actual index @. Objecthood, situationhood, the actual world, and meet and join are defined. Formally:

enc : W × P → Prop ⊨ : W × Π → Prop Ob : W × Π → Prop E : W × W → Prop VAC : Π → P @ : W

Together with the propositional operations on Π, these exhaust the primitive vocabulary. Every subsequent notion is either postulated explicitly or introduced by definition.

Constitutive Principles

The development rests upon three constitutive principles governing the interaction between objects, properties, propositions, and indices.

First, truth in an object is reducible to encoding:

x ⊨ p ↔ enc(x,VAC(p))

Second, obtaining at an index is classical, and equal vacuous properties have the same obtaining conditions (in Zalta's theory, a consequence of β-conversion):

Ob(v,¬p) ↔ ¬Ob(v,p)   Ob(v,p ∧ q) ↔ Ob(v,p) ∧ Ob(v,q) VAC(p) = VAC(q) → (Ob(v,p) ↔ Ob(v,q))

Third, abstract objects obey Zalta's comprehension and identity principles, and ordinary objects encode nothing:

∃x(A!x ∧ ∀F(enc(x,F) ↔ φ(F)))   A!x ∧ A!y ∧ ∀F(enc(x,F) ↔ enc(y,F)) → x = y

These principles, with the propositional identities, constitute the entire bridge between the propositional, ontological and modal layers of the theory. That situations are objects, that they encode only vacuous abstractions, that they are individuated by their truths, and that the actual world and meets behave as intended are all theorems.

Definitional Extensions

Objects and situations are defined as in Zalta, from ordinariness (possible concreteness) and abstractness:

Obj(x) :⟺ O!x ∨ A!x   Sit(x) :⟺ A!x ∧ ∀F(enc(x,F) → ∃p(F = VAC(p)))

Parthood is introduced as inclusion in encoded content:

s ⊴ s′ :⟺ ∀F(enc(s,F) → enc(s′,F))

Accessibility is defined universally:

R(w,v) :⟺ ⊤

Necessity and possibility are introduced semantically:

□φ(w) :⟺ ∀v(R(w,v) → φ(v)) ◊φ(w) :⟺ ¬□¬φ(w)

Ontological dependence is defined by preservation across all containing situations:

s ≺ s′ :⟺ ∀u(Sit(u) → s ⊴ u → s′ ⊴ u)

Worldhood, actuality and possibility follow Zalta, with obtaining at the index of evaluation in place of truth simpliciter:

World(s) :⟺ Sit(s) ∧ ◊(λv. ∀p(s ⊨ p ↔ Ob(v,p)))(@) Actual(s) :⟺ ∀p(s ⊨ p → Ob(@,p)) Possible(s) :⟺ ◊(λv. ∀p(s ⊨ p → Ob(v,p)))(@)

The actuality operator evaluates a condition at the actual index, whatever the index of evaluation:

𝒜φ(w) :⟺ φ(@)

Modal Consequences

Because accessibility is definitionally universal, the modal operators reduce to unrestricted quantification over the world domain. Reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity are therefore consequences of definition rather than independent frame conditions. The normal modal principles K, T, 4, and 5, together with necessitation and the characteristic S5 principle,

◊□φ → □φ

are all established as theorems. The modal strength of the system is thus generated by a single definitional commitment concerning accessibility, rather than by a collection of independent modal axioms.

Situation-Theoretic Consequences

The central theorem of the development identifies the mereological and truth-theoretic orderings:

s ⊴ s′ ↔ ∀p(s ⊨ p → s′ ⊨ p)

This result establishes that inclusion of encoded content coincides exactly with inclusion of propositional content. From it follow antisymmetry of parthood, identity by common parts, and persistence of truth under inclusion.

s ⊴ s′ ∧ s′ ⊴ s → s = s′ ∀u(u ⊴ s ↔ u ⊴ s′) → s = s′ s ⊨ p ∧ s ⊴ s′ → s′ ⊨ p

World-Theoretic Consequences

Maximality and consistency of worlds are not assumed but derived. A world matches some index on every proposition, and so inherits the classical truth conditions of obtaining; in particular it decides every proposition and contains no contradiction.

World(w) → ∀p(w ⊨ p ∨ w ⊨ ¬p) World(w) → ¬∃p(w ⊨ p ∧ w ⊨ ¬p)

Consistency follows already from possibility, and the worlds are exactly the maximal possible situations:

Possible(s) → ¬∃p(s ⊨ p ∧ s ⊨ ¬p) World(s) ↔ Sit(s) ∧ ∀p(s ⊨ p ∨ s ⊨ ¬p) ∧ Possible(s)

The actual world is not postulated. It is the abstract object that encodes exactly the vacuous abstractions of what obtains at the actual index, and that its truths are exactly what obtains there is a theorem; from it follow that the actual world is a world and is actual. No proper part of a world is itself a world.

Sit(w@) ∧ ∀p(w@ ⊨ p ↔ Ob(@,p))

Lattice Structure

The parthood relation organizes the domain of situations into an ordered algebraic structure. Reflexivity is immediate from the definition of ⊴; antisymmetry is the identity-by-common-parts theorem; transitivity descends from the transitivity of implication in the encoding clause. The situations therefore constitute a partial order ⟨Sit, ⊴⟩, whose covering relation is displayed below.

The equivalence of parthood and truth-inclusion shows this order to be isomorphic to the inclusion order on truth-sets. Writing ⟦s⟧ = { p : s ⊨ p } for the propositional content of a situation, the central theorem reads:

s ⊴ s′  ↔  ⟦s⟧ ⊆ ⟦s′⟧

Parthood is therefore not merely analogous to but identical with propositional containment, and the entire order may be recovered from the family of truth-sets. Binary meets are defined by comprehension, as the abstract object encoding what both situations encode, and their truth condition is a theorem — the meet of two situations makes true precisely those propositions made true by both:

s ∧ s′ ⊨ p  ↔  (s ⊨ p ∧ s′ ⊨ p)

Under the truth-set isomorphism this condition is exactly set intersection, ⟦s ∧ s′⟧ = ⟦s⟧ ∩ ⟦s′⟧, as the following diagram records.

That s ∧ s′ is a lower bound, and the greatest such, then follows immediately: the two inequalities and the universal property are derived theorems rather than further postulates, and the structure ⟨Sit, ⊴, ∧⟩ is a meet-semilattice. Binary joins are defined in the same way, so the order is in fact a lattice, but arbitrary joins are not assumed. Worlds are maximal among the possible situations — a world decides every proposition and harbours no contradiction, so no proper part of a world is itself a world, and no possible situation properly extends one. They are not maximal outright: the situation that makes everything true lies above every world, but it is impossible.

Actuality and Contingent Logical Truth

Let a model of propositional modal logic with actuality be a Kripke model with a designated actual world @, in which 𝒜φ is true at any world just in case φ is true at @. A formula is real-world valid in a class of models if it is true at the actual world of each, and generally valid if it is true at every world of each (Crossley and Humberstone; Davies and Humberstone). For an arbitrary class of models, general validity implies real-world validity and is closed under necessitation, while the actuality biconditional is real-world valid for every formula:

⊨g φ ⟹ ⊨g □φ    ⊨rw φ ↔ 𝒜φ

Its necessitation holds at a world exactly when φ takes its actual value at every accessible world, and so fails wherever φ is contingent. On every class containing a two-world model, including the S5 models, real-world validity is therefore not closed under necessitation and properly extends general validity.

𝔐, w ⊨ □(φ ↔ 𝒜φ)  ↔  ∀v(wRv → (𝔐, v ⊨ φ ↔ 𝔐, @ ⊨ φ))

Prefixing 𝒜 converts real-world validity into general validity, so that 𝒜(φ ↔ 𝒜φ) is generally valid and hence necessary: what is necessary is not the biconditional but that it actually holds. In the situation theory the actuality operator is definable, its necessitation holds exactly for necessary or impossible conditions, and the theory, read as a model of the logic, validates every real-world valid formula at its actual world. If logical truth is real-world validity, the theory thus contains logical truths that are not necessary, as Zalta (1988) argued.

The same comparison extends to first-order modal logic with variable domains, where the quantifier ranges over what exists at the world of evaluation. The closure results and the characterization carry over under every assignment, and the domains add contingent logical truths of their own. “Everything exists”, ∀y E!y, is generally valid and hence necessary. “Everything actually exists”, ∀y 𝒜E!y, is real-world valid, but its necessitation holds at a world exactly when no accessible world has an object that does not actually exist:

𝔐, w ⊨ □∀y 𝒜E!y  ↔  ∀v(wRv → d(v) ⊆ d(@))

Consistency

The theory has a model. Take two valuations, of which one is actual; let propositions and properties be sets of valuations carrying a tag bit, with the connectives acting on the sets and combining the tags; let the objects be one index for each valuation, which is concrete and encodes nothing, together with one abstract object for each set of properties. Truth in a situation is encoding, and obtaining at an index is membership of its valuation, which ignores the tag. Every axiom of the theory holds in this structure, and the model is non-degenerate:

PropertyWitness
Two distinct worldsthe situations true at either valuation
A contingent propositionthe set containing only the actual valuation
A partial, possible situation below a worldthe situation encoding nothing
An impossible situationthe situation encoding everything
Ordinary and abstract objectsthe indices and the encoding objects
Distinct but necessarily equivalent propositionsthe contingent proposition with either tag

In this model the actuality biconditional for the contingent proposition is true at the actual world but not necessary. The correspondence between the model and the axioms is verified mechanically.

Resolved Proof Obligations

OP-1 Necessitation and the S5 characteristic thesis

The necessitation rule (⊢ φ ⟹ ⊢ □φ) and the S5 collapse (◊□φ → □φ) are not derivable from the schemas K, T, 4, 5 alone. Both were initially postulated. They are now derived consequences of the definitional choice Rwv :⟺ ⊤: necessitation holds because □φ(w) reduces to ∀v. φ(v), and the S5 collapse holds because any ◊-witness already has □φ, which at the universal relation yields φ everywhere.

OP-2 Parthood–truth equivalence

The direction ∀p. (s ⊨ p → s′ ⊨ p) → s ⊴ s′ was not derivable given that ⊴ is defined over encoding while ⊨ is an independent primitive. The gap is closed by the principle that situations encode only vacuous abstractions: given truth-inclusion, any property encoded by s is of the form VAC(p), and truth-inclusion then transfers encoding directly. This yields the full biconditional of Theorem 4 and unlocks Theorems 5, 6, and 8.

OP-2b Dependence and parthood

Dependence runs from a situation to its parts: s′ ⊴ s implies s ≺ s′ by transitivity of parthood, so a whole inherits the dependencies of its parts. The converse, that a part depends on its wholes, is not derivable, and postulating it would identify every situation with each of its parts.

OP-3 Situations are objects

The reduction of truth to encoding requires the object predicate as a hypothesis. Since situationhood and objecthood are independent primitives, their overlap had to be postulated: Sit(s) → Obj(s). With this in place, the monotonicity direction of Theorem 4 closes without any open obligation. Situations and objects have since been defined as in Zalta, and the inclusion is now a theorem.

OP-4 Lattice structure of meet

The meet is characterised truth-theoretically: s ∧ s′ ⊨ p iff s ⊨ p and s′ ⊨ p. The lattice properties — that the meet is a lower bound and the greatest such — require translating between the truth-based characterisation and the encoding-based definition of ⊴. This translation is exactly the content of Theorem 4, and all three lattice properties follow immediately once that theorem is available.

OP-5 No strict subworld

No proper part of a world is itself a world. The proof proceeds by maximality: if s ⊴ w and both are worlds, then for any proposition p, the maximality of w and the monotonicity of truth under ⊴ force s and w to agree on every proposition, whence extensionality yields s = w, contradicting strict parthood. The two cases — forward and backward agreement — each reduce to a single application of truth-monotonicity or a classical argument through maximality.
